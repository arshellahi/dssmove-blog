Moving into temporary housing is stressful. For families with children, it can also disrupt schooling, healthcare, and access to support. A new rule that started on 1 September 2026 aims to make that disruption smaller.

## What your council must now do

Under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, your local housing authority must ask your permission to contact:

- your child's school or nursery
- your GP practice
- the health visiting service in your area

Your council should ask you as soon as you are placed in temporary accommodation. If you agree, they have 14 days to send the notification to each relevant service.

You do not have to agree. But saying yes means professionals who already support your child will be told straight away that your family has moved.

## Why this matters

Before this rule, families often had to make every call themselves. A child could wait weeks before a teacher or health visitor knew they had moved. That gap made it harder to get support quickly.

Now, the notification puts the right people in the picture without you having to track down every contact. Your child's school can begin transferring records. Your GP can update your address. Your health visitor can get in touch if your child is under 5.

You can still contact services yourself as well. Calling your child's school or GP directly is always a good idea if you need something urgently.

## Who this rule covers

The duty applies across England. Around 177,530 children were living in temporary accommodation in England in 2026 — the highest number on record.

It applies to placements made on or after 1 September 2026. If your family was placed in temporary accommodation on or after that date, your council should already have asked you about notifications.

The rule covers children placed under the main homelessness duty. It also covers 16 and 17 year olds who make their own homelessness application.

## If your council does not ask

If your council places you in temporary accommodation but does not mention notifications, ask your housing officer directly. You can say: "I want you to send notifications to my child's school, GP practice, and health visiting service."

If they do not act, Citizens Advice can help you understand your options. Shelter also offers free advice and legal guidance for families in temporary accommodation.

## Does this apply in Scotland, Wales, or Northern Ireland?

No. The duty sits in English law. For support in Scotland, Wales, or Northern Ireland, contact Citizens Advice or Shelter in your part of the UK.
