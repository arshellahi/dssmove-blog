## What is the carer's element?

If you claim Universal Credit (UC) and spend at least 35 hours a week looking after a disabled person, you could get an extra £209.34 a month. This is called the carer's element.

Many people who care for a family member or friend do not know about this payment. It is worth checking — that money could make a big difference to your rent and bills.

## Who can get it?

There are 2 things that need to be true.

**You must spend at least 35 hours a week caring for someone.** You do not need to live with them.

**The person you care for must receive a qualifying disability payment.** The most common ones are:

- The daily living component of Personal Independence Payment (PIP)
- The highest or middle rate care component of Disability Living Allowance (DLA)
- Attendance Allowance at any rate

The GOV.UK page for Universal Credit lists all qualifying disability payments.

## How to report it to the DWP

The carer's element is not added to your UC automatically. You need to tell the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) that you are a carer.

Here is what to do:

1. Sign in to your UC account at gov.uk.
2. Open your online journal.
3. Send a message to your work coach. Say you are a carer, and give the name, date of birth, and disability benefit of the person you look after.
4. The DWP will check and update your award.

The extra money will appear on your next UC payment date.

## Do you need Carer's Allowance too?

Carer's Allowance is a different benefit. You do not have to claim it to get the carer's element inside UC.

If you do get Carer's Allowance, it counts as income and will reduce your UC by the same amount. The carer's element is different — it sits on top of your standard UC payment and is not clawed back like that.

If you are unsure which works out better for you, Citizens Advice can help you work it out for free.

## One important rule

You cannot get the carer's element and the UC health element — called Limited Capability for Work-Related Activity (LCWRA) — at the same time. If you think you may qualify for both, your DWP work coach can tell you which gives you more in your situation.

There is no earnings limit for the carer's element. You can work any number of hours and still receive it, as long as you care for someone for at least 35 hours a week.
