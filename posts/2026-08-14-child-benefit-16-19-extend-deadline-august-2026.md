Your Child Benefit stops automatically on 31 August if you do not tell HMRC that your teenager is staying in full-time education or approved training. The deadline is 17 days away — act now to keep the money coming in.

## Why your Child Benefit is at risk

Child Benefit is paid for children up to their 16th birthday. After that, it stops on 31 August unless you extend your claim.

This affects you if your teenager:

- is returning to school or college in September for A levels, T levels, GCSEs, or a similar course
- is starting an approved, unpaid training programme

If you do nothing, Child Benefit stops — even if your child is going straight back into education in autumn.

Around 1.5 million reminder letters were sent to families from May 2026. But you do not need to wait for a letter. You can extend today.

## Who qualifies for continued payments

Your teenager qualifies if they are in **full-time non-advanced education** — at least 12 hours of lessons and practical work each week during term time. Qualifying courses include:

- A levels, T levels, and GCSEs
- Scottish Highers and Advanced Highers
- SVQs and NVQs at level 3 or below

**Approved training** must be unpaid and not part of a job contract. Paid apprenticeships do not qualify and Child Benefit will stop.

Payments can continue until 31 August after your teenager's 19th birthday, as long as they remain in qualifying education or training throughout.

## How to extend before 31 August

Use the **HMRC app** or sign in at GOV.UK. It only takes a few minutes. If you cannot go online, call HMRC using the phone number on your renewal letter.

Act before 31 August even if your teenager has not yet received exam results or confirmed their September course. You can update HMRC later if plans change.

## If you also claim Universal Credit

Child Benefit and Universal Credit are run by different departments. Extending your Child Benefit does not update your Universal Credit automatically.

Log in to your **UC online journal** and report that your teenager is continuing in qualifying education or training. If you do not, DWP may reduce the child element that is added to your monthly Universal Credit payment.

If you are unsure what to put, contact Citizens Advice — an adviser can help you update both claims correctly at no cost to you.
