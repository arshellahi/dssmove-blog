If you live in a private rented home in England and have been dealing with damp, mould, or other dangerous conditions, a new government consultation could soon mean your landlord has a legal deadline to fix it.

The consultation opened on 9 October 2026 and closes on 18 December 2026. It is the last step before the government writes the regulations that will bring Awaab's Law to private rented homes.

## What is Awaab's Law?

Awaab's Law is named after Awaab Ishak, a toddler who died in 2020 after living with severe mould in a housing association property. His death led to a new law requiring social landlords — councils and housing associations — to investigate and fix dangerous hazards within set legal timeframes.

Since the law came in, social landlords in England have had to act quickly when tenants report hazards like damp, mould, excess cold, or fire risks. Private landlords have not had the same obligation — until now.

## What the consultation means for private renters

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 already gives the government the power to apply Awaab's Law to private rented homes. What the consultation is deciding is the detail:

- which specific hazards the timescales will cover
- how many days private landlords will have to investigate and carry out repairs
- whether the rules will also apply to temporary accommodation and supported housing

The consultation runs until 18 December 2026. Once it closes, the government will write regulations setting the exact requirements for private landlords.

## What this will mean once the rules are in place

Right now, if you report a hazard to a private landlord, there is no legal deadline for them to act. They can ignore you for weeks or months without breaking the law.

Once Awaab's Law applies to private rented homes, your landlord will have a fixed number of days to investigate and carry out repairs. That deadline will be set in law — not left up to your landlord.

## What you can do right now

The new rules are not in force yet. But you do not have to wait to get help.

If your home has damp, mould, cold, or another serious hazard:

1. **Write to your landlord** asking them to fix the problem. Keep a copy of your message and the date you sent it.
2. **Contact your local council** and ask for a free Housing Health and Safety Rating System (HHSRS) inspection. If the inspector finds a serious hazard, your council can legally require your landlord to act.
3. **Get free advice** from Citizens Advice (citizensadvice.org.uk) or Shelter (shelter.org.uk). Both services are free and can help you work out your options.

You do not need to wait for the new regulations. These routes are available to you today.
