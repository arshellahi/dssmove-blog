If you claim Universal Credit (UC) or another means-tested benefit, you may be paying more for your internet or phone than you need to. A social tariff is a discounted deal offered by major broadband and mobile providers to people on qualifying benefits. Switching could save you up to £180 a year.

## What is a social tariff?

A social tariff is a cut-price broadband or mobile package for people on low incomes. Providers set their own prices, but many charge from around £10 to £15 a month — roughly half what a standard deal costs. You get the same speeds and reliability as a regular plan.

Social tariffs are not a government benefit. Private providers run them. But a growing number of the UK's biggest names offer one, and more are joining all the time.

## Who qualifies?

You can get a social tariff if someone in your household receives:

- Universal Credit
- Pension Credit
- Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)
- Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA)
- Income Support

Some providers accept a wider list, including Personal Independence Payment (PIP). It is always worth asking your provider directly, even if you are not certain you qualify.

The person named on the benefit usually needs to be the main account holder.

## How to apply

Contact your broadband or mobile provider and ask if they offer a social tariff. Most let you apply online or over the phone.

DWP now runs an automatic check system. With your permission, your provider can contact DWP to confirm your benefit status in seconds. That means you do not need to dig out letters or print anything off. If the automatic check is not available, your UC award letter or payment statement will usually be enough.

Switching to a social tariff — even if you move to a new provider to do it — is usually free of charge.

## If your provider does not have a social tariff

You are not stuck. You can switch to a provider that does.

Citizens Advice has free guidance on comparing broadband deals and can help you switch if you are not confident doing it yourself. The Ofcom website keeps a full and regularly updated list of providers and their social tariff prices, so you can see what is available in your area.

## What to do next

Call or go online to your provider today and ask one question: do you have a social tariff? If they do, switching usually takes just a few minutes. If they do not, Citizens Advice can help you find one that does.
