## What is the UC carer element?

If you look after someone who is disabled or has a long-term health condition, Universal Credit can add an extra £209.34 a month to your payments. This is called the carer element.

You do not have to stop working to receive it. There is no earnings limit on the carer element. You can earn any amount and still get it — unlike some other carer benefits.

But DWP will not add it to your payments automatically. You have to tell them that you are a carer.

## Who qualifies?

You can get the carer element if:

- You are already on Universal Credit
- You spend at least 35 hours a week caring for someone
- The person you care for gets one of these benefits:
  - Personal Independence Payment (PIP) — the daily living part
  - Disability Living Allowance (DLA) — the middle or highest care rate
  - Attendance Allowance
  - Constant Attendance Allowance

You do not have to live with the person you care for.

If you and a partner both care for the same person, only one of you can receive the carer element.

## How to claim it

1. Sign in to your Universal Credit online account.
2. Open your journal and report that you are providing care.
3. DWP will update your claim — the extra amount should appear from your next assessment period.

If you are not yet on Universal Credit, you can report your caring role when you first apply.

## What about Carer's Allowance?

Carer's Allowance is a separate benefit. It pays £86.45 a week if you care for someone for 35 or more hours a week and your net earnings are below £204 a week.

If you receive Carer's Allowance, your Universal Credit award is reduced by the same amount. Even so, it is usually worth claiming. It builds up National Insurance credits, and if your Universal Credit stops for any reason, Carer's Allowance will keep coming in.

You do not need to claim Carer's Allowance before you can get the UC carer element. They are entirely separate.

## Check your account now

Many carers on Universal Credit are missing this payment simply because they have not told DWP about their caring role. If you look after a disabled friend or family member, check your UC journal today.

If you are unsure what you are entitled to, Citizens Advice can give you free, confidential help.
