If you rent privately and were born on or before 27 June 1960, you could get between £200 and £300 to help with your heating bills this winter. The money is called the Winter Fuel Payment, and most eligible people receive it without having to apply.

The payment is not means-tested. You do not need to be on Housing Benefit, Universal Credit, or Pension Credit to receive it. You just need to be the right age and to have been living in England, Wales, or Northern Ireland during the week of 21 to 27 September 2026.

## How much will you get?

The amount depends on when you were born. If you live alone, or no one else you live with also qualifies:

- **£300** if you were born before 28 September 1946
- **£200** if you were born between 28 September 1946 and 27 June 1960

If you share a home with another eligible person, the payment is split between you. Check GOV.UK for the exact rates in your situation.

## Will you be paid automatically?

Most people are paid without needing to do anything. Look out for a letter in October or November 2026 telling you how much you will receive. Payment usually arrives in November or December 2026.

You only need to make a claim if:

- you have never received a Winter Fuel Payment before, or
- you have deferred your State Pension since you last got the payment

Phone claims open on 19 October 2026. The deadline to claim is 31 March 2027.

## What if your income is over £35,000?

If your total taxable income is more than £35,000 a year, HMRC will take the payment back by adjusting your tax code. This happens in the 2027 to 2028 tax year, so you will not be asked to pay anything back from your bank account directly.

You cannot opt out of the 2026 to 2027 payment, even if your income is over the limit. If you want to stop getting the payment in future winters, you can opt out from 21 December 2026 for the following year.

## What to do now

If you got a Winter Fuel Payment last winter, sit tight — your letter should arrive soon. Check the amount it states matches what you expect using GOV.UK's rate table.

If you are pension age and have never received the payment, visit GOV.UK or contact Citizens Advice to find out how to make a claim before the March 2027 deadline.

If you are also on a low income, it is worth checking whether you are entitled to Pension Credit. Many private renters at pension age are missing out. Claiming Pension Credit can top up your weekly income and help with your rent — Citizens Advice offers a free benefits check to see what you are owed.
