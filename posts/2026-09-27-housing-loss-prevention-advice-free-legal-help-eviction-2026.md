If your landlord or letting agent has given you a written notice to leave, you can get free legal help straight away. Most people do not know this service exists until it is too late to use it fully.

## What is the Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service?

The Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service (HLPAS) is a free, government-funded scheme. It connects you with a qualified solicitor the moment your landlord starts the eviction process. You do not have to wait for a court date before you can ask for help.

The solicitor can advise you on three areas:

- **Housing** — whether the notice is valid, what your landlord can do next, and what your rights are
- **Debt** — if rent arrears are part of the reason for the notice, a solicitor can help you work out a repayment plan with your landlord
- **Benefits** — if your Universal Credit or Housing Benefit needs updating, or you think you have been paid the wrong amount, the solicitor can advise on this too

## When can you use it?

You can ask for help as soon as your landlord hands you a written notice. Since May 2026, all private landlords in England and Wales must use a section 8 notice if they want you to leave. Receiving that notice is your trigger to act.

If the case goes further and your landlord applies to court, the same scheme provides free representation on the day. A solicitor will be at the court and can speak for you if you have not had time to prepare.

## Is there an income or savings limit?

No. The Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service is not means-tested. It does not matter how much you earn, whether you are in work, or how much you have in savings. If you are at risk of losing your home, you are eligible.

This is different from most legal aid. You do not have to fill in a financial form before you can get advice.

## How to find a provider

Go to the GOV.UK page listed below. There is a postcode search tool on the page. Type in your postcode and it will show your nearest approved providers. Contact one directly — most offer phone or in-person appointments.

The earlier you get advice after receiving a notice, the more options you are likely to have. Do not wait.

If you are unsure where to start, Citizens Advice and Shelter can both help you find free housing advice in your area.
