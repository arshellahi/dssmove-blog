## What is the Crisis and Resilience Fund?

The **Crisis and Resilience Fund (CRF)** launched on 1 April 2026. It replaced the Household Support Fund, which ended in March 2026. The new fund runs until March 2029.

Every council in England receives government money to run the fund. Your council decides how to manage it locally, so the exact rules and amounts can vary. But the basic idea is the same everywhere: if you cannot afford essentials, your council can help.

## What can the fund pay for?

The CRF can cover a wide range of costs, including:

- **food** — vouchers or cash for groceries
- **energy bills** — help with gas, electricity, oil, or prepayment meter top-ups
- **water bills**
- **clothing and school uniforms**
- **household items** you cannot do without, such as a cooker or a fridge

Some councils also use part of the fund to help with rent or moving costs. This is called a **CRF Housing Payment**. If you need help with rent specifically, read our guide on [rent help through a CRF Housing Payment](/apply-for-discretionary-housing-payment/).

## Who can apply?

You do **not** need to be on benefits. The fund is open to anyone on a low income who is facing a financial crisis.

If you already claim Universal Credit (UC) or Housing Benefit, a CRF payment will not reduce what you get. It is a grant and does not need to be repaid.

You do need to live in England. Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland each run their own emergency support schemes.

## How do you apply?

Contact your local council directly. Most councils let you apply online, by phone, or in person.

To find your council, search on GOV.UK for "find your local council". Or use the **Turn2us Grants Search** — enter your postcode and it will show what help is available near you.

There is **no deadline**. Applications are open all year. If your need is urgent, most councils aim to respond within 48 hours.

## What happens next?

Your council will look at your situation. They may ask for evidence, such as a bank statement or a recent bill.

Help usually arrives as **vouchers** (for example, supermarket vouchers), a **direct cash payment**, or goods such as a food parcel or a prepayment meter top-up.

If the council says no, you can ask them to review the decision. Citizens Advice can help you challenge a refusal for free.
