Universal Credit (UC) standard allowance went up in April 2026. This is the core monthly payment that every UC claimant gets, before extras for rent, children, or health conditions are added. If you are on UC, your base payment should now be higher.

## What changed and when

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) raised the standard allowance from 6 April 2026. Rates went up by around 6% in total — 3.8% to match rising prices, plus an extra 2.3% added on top of that.

## Your new monthly amounts

The amount you get depends on your age and whether you have a partner.

- **Single, under 25:** £338.58 a month (was £316.98)
- **Single, 25 or over:** £424.90 a month (was £400.14)
- **Couple, both under 25:** £528.34 a month (was £497.55)
- **Couple, both 25 or over:** £666.97 a month (was £628.10)

These are the standard allowance figures only. Your actual UC payment may be higher if you also get a housing element to help with rent, a child element for each child, or an extra amount because of a health condition or disability.

If you have a partner and you are different ages, use the Turn2us benefits calculator to work out the right figure for your household.

## When you will see the increase

UC runs in monthly assessment periods. The new rates apply from the first period that started on or after 6 April 2026. Most people will have seen the higher amount land in May 2026.

If your assessment period begins later in the month, your first increased payment may not arrive until June. You can see when your current period started by logging into your UC account on GOV.UK.

## How to check your payments

Open your payment statement in your UC account. Find the line that shows your standard allowance. It should match the figure above for your situation. While you are there, check your other elements too — for example, that your housing element is still covering the right amount toward your rent.

## If something looks wrong

If the figures do not add up, contact Citizens Advice. They offer free, confidential help and can go through your statement with you. If DWP has made a mistake, they can help you challenge it.

Shelter also offers free advice if you are worried that a payment problem could put your rent at risk.
