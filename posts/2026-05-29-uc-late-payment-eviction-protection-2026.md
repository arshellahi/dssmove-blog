## What changed on 1 May 2026

If you rent privately in England and receive Universal Credit (UC), new rules now protect you if a payment delay makes you fall behind on rent.

Courts in England must now disregard any rent arrears caused by a UC housing payment that has been agreed by DWP but not yet arrived. If your housing costs entitlement has been confirmed, those weeks cannot count as genuine arrears against you in court.

This is a significant change. UC often takes 5 weeks to arrive when you first claim. Payments can also be delayed by changes to your circumstances. Under the old rules, those delayed weeks could still count against you if your landlord took you to court. Under the new rules, they cannot.

## The 3-month threshold

Before 1 May 2026, a landlord could issue a mandatory eviction notice once you owed 2 months' rent on a monthly tenancy. That threshold has now risen to 3 months' rent — or 13 weeks on a weekly tenancy.

This gives you more breathing space if you fall behind. You have more time to catch up, speak to your landlord, or get advice from Citizens Advice before the matter reaches a court.

Your landlord must also give you at least 4 weeks' notice before applying to court — up from 2 weeks before.

If a hearing does go ahead, a judge can pause the case to wait for DWP to confirm your housing costs entitlement. The aim of these changes is to make sure a short-term benefit delay does not cost you your home.

## What to do if your rent is behind

Do not wait if you know your rent is overdue.

**Check your UC journal** to see whether your housing payment has been processed and when it is due. If DWP has agreed to pay your housing costs but the money has not arrived, make a note of this.

**Tell your landlord as soon as possible.** Most landlords would rather sort things out than go to court. Let them know UC is delayed and show them any communication from DWP.

**Ask about Alternative Payment Arrangements (APA).** DWP can in some cases pay your rent directly to your landlord. This can stop arrears from building up in the first place.

**Contact Citizens Advice** for free, confidential help if you are worried about arrears or a potential eviction. They can check whether your UC claim is correct, help you communicate with your landlord, and explain what your rights are.

**Shelter** also runs a free housing helpline if you are worried about losing your home.
