The government's independent review of Personal Independence Payment (PIP) published its interim findings in July 2026. It is the first major look at PIP since the benefit launched in 2013.

The headline finding: PIP is **"not fit for purpose."**

That sounds worrying. But the review was set up to fix problems, not take PIP away. Here is what it means for you.

## What is PIP?

PIP helps with the extra costs of living with a long-term health condition or disability. It is not means-tested — it does not depend on how much you earn. You can get PIP alongside Universal Credit (UC).

If you pay rent and have a disability or long-term condition, PIP can help cover things like adapted equipment or higher transport costs that renting often involves.

## What the review found

More than 38,000 people responded to the review's call for evidence. The message was consistent:

- PIP is valued. It helps cover real costs that other benefits do not.
- The assessment process is often **"stressful and dehumanising."**
- The way PIP works has not kept up with how disability and health are understood today.

The review was led by Sir Stephen Timms, who also heard from disability organisations, carers, and health professionals across the country.

## What might change?

The review team is now working on recommendations. These will go to the Secretary of State by autumn 2026. No changes have happened yet.

The aim is to make assessments fairer, more accurate, and less distressing — not to cut support for people who genuinely need it.

## What this review does not do

This review is separate from the UC and PIP Bill changes. Those changes set new rules for who qualifies for PIP's daily living component from November 2026, and they apply to **new claims only** — not existing awards.

The Timms Review is about improving the experience for everyone who goes through an assessment.

## What to do now

If you already get PIP, you do not need to do anything because of this review.

If you are worried about a current PIP claim, a renewal, or a decision you disagree with, get free support now:

- **Citizens Advice** can help you challenge a decision or prepare for a review.
- **Shelter** can help if a benefit problem is affecting your ability to keep your home.
