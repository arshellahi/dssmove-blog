## Why this right matters to you

Having a pet at home can make a huge difference to your wellbeing. But for years, many landlords have used blanket "no pets" clauses that gave tenants no way to ask.

From **1 May 2026**, that changed in England. Under the Renters' Rights Act 2025, you now have the legal right to ask your landlord to let you keep a pet. They cannot simply say no without giving you a proper reason.

## How to make a pet request

Put your request in writing. A letter or email is fine. Your request should include:

- a description of the pet you want to keep
- your name and the address of the property

Keep a copy of what you send, and note the date.

Your landlord then has **28 days** to reply in writing. If they ask you for more details about the pet, they still have to give you an answer within 28 days of your original request.

## What your landlord can and cannot do

Your landlord can agree or refuse. But if they refuse, they must tell you the reason in writing. A blanket "no pets" clause in your tenancy agreement is not enough on its own anymore.

There may be times when a refusal is reasonable — for example, if the freehold lease of the building bans pets. In that case, your landlord should show you evidence of this.

If your landlord does not reply within 28 days, you have the right to apply to court.

## Who this right covers

This right applies to people renting privately in England under an **assured periodic tenancy**. Most private renters in England automatically moved to this type of tenancy on 1 May 2026.

If you rent from a social landlord — such as a housing association or council — this right does not apply in the same way. Contact your social landlord directly to ask about their pets policy.

Not sure what type of tenancy you have? Check your tenancy agreement or ask Citizens Advice for free.

## A simple way to make the request

There is no official form. A short email works well. You could write:

*"I am writing to request permission to keep a [type of pet] at [address] under the Renters' Rights Act 2025."*

Keep any reply your landlord sends. If they say no, read the reason carefully. If you think the refusal is unfair, speak to [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/) for free help.
