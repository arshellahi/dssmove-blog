If you rent privately in England, you can now ask your landlord if you can keep a pet. This right came into force on 1 May 2026 under the Renters' Rights Act 2025.

Before this change, many landlords added a blanket "no pets" clause to tenancy agreements. They could refuse without giving a reason. That has now changed for private renters.

## How to make your request

You must ask in writing. An email works, as long as you keep a copy.

Your request needs to describe the pet. Include the type of animal, its breed, and its age or size. For example: "I would like to keep a 2-year-old male black Labrador."

You do not need to use any special form. A clear email to your landlord or letting agent is enough.

Keep a record of when you sent the request. This matters because of the response deadlines below.

## What your landlord must do

Your landlord has 28 days to respond in writing after they get your request.

If they ask you for more details about the pet within those 28 days, they get an extra 7 days to give you their final answer.

Your landlord must not refuse your request without a good reason.

## What counts as a good reason to refuse

Your landlord can reasonably say no in some situations. For example:

- Another tenant in the building is allergic to the type of pet you want
- The property is too small for the animal
- The pet would be illegal to own in England
- The building is leasehold and the freeholder does not allow pets

Your landlord cannot refuse just because they do not like pets. A general worry about damage to the property is also not a good enough reason on its own. The law requires landlords to look at each request on its own merits.

## No extra charges allowed

Your landlord cannot charge you more rent because you have a pet. They also cannot ask for a higher deposit. The deposit cap still applies — your deposit cannot exceed 5 weeks' rent, whether or not you have a pet.

If your landlord tries to add a pet fee or increase your rent as a condition of agreeing, you do not have to accept it.

## What to do if your request is refused

Ask your landlord to explain their reason in writing.

If you believe the refusal is unreasonable, you can make a formal complaint or apply to court. A court can order your landlord to allow the pet if it decides the refusal was unfair.

For free advice on your next steps, contact [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk) or [Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk).
