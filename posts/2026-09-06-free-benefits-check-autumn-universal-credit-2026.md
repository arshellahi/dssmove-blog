The new school year has just started. Winter energy bills are a few months away. Both are good reasons to check you are getting all the support you are entitled to.

A free online check takes under 10 minutes. It may show benefits you are not claiming, or that your current payment is lower than it could be.

## Why September is a good time to check

Several things changed in April 2026 that could mean you are now entitled to more.

The **two-child limit was removed on 6 April 2026**. If you have three or more children and claim Universal Credit, you can now get a child element for each of them. If you had a child recently, or did not tell the DWP about a third or later child, your payment may not yet reflect this.

The Universal Credit **standard allowance also increased in April 2026** — from £400.14 to £424.90 a month for a single person aged 25 or over. If you have not checked your entitlement since last year, a calculator will use the updated figures.

With winter approaching, it is also worth checking whether you qualify for the **Warm Home Discount** or **Cold Weather Payments** before those qualifying windows open later in the year.

## How to check for free

The GOV.UK benefits calculator page (gov.uk/benefits-calculators) lists the free tools recommended by the government.

The **Turn2us benefits calculator** covers Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, Council Tax Reduction, Child Benefit, and Carer's Allowance. You will need to know your monthly take-home pay, your rent, your savings, and your Council Tax amount. It takes around 10 minutes.

**entitledto** offers a similar check and can pick up some local-authority schemes that other calculators miss. Both are free to use.

To get the most accurate result, have recent payslips, a copy of your tenancy agreement, and your bank statements to hand before you start.

## Getting personalised help

If your situation is complicated — for example, you are self-employed, you care for someone, or you have irregular income — **Citizens Advice** can give you free, personalised help.

You do not need an appointment at most Citizens Advice offices. You can also call the free Adviceline on **0800 144 8848** (Monday to Friday) or get help online at citizensadvice.org.uk.

## What to do next

If a check shows you could be getting more:

- **Universal Credit claimants**: report the change in your online journal or ask your work coach.
- **Housing Benefit claimants**: contact your local council's benefits team.
- **Not yet claiming**: you can apply at gov.uk/apply-universal-credit or call the UC helpline on **0800 328 5644**.

If you think a past DWP decision was wrong, you have the right to request a mandatory reconsideration. Citizens Advice can guide you through that process for free.
