If you rent privately in England, you now have the right to ask your landlord if you can keep a pet. This came into force on 1 May 2026 under the Renters' Rights Act. Your landlord can still say no — but only if they have a good reason.

## What this change means for you

Before May 2026, landlords could add a blanket "no pets" rule to your tenancy agreement. That is no longer allowed. You now have the legal right to ask, and your landlord must consider your request properly.

If they say no, they must tell you why in writing. A blanket refusal is not good enough.

## How to make your request

Write to your landlord. An email, a text, or a letter all count — as long as it is in writing. You should:

- Say that you want to keep a pet.
- Describe the pet — for example, "a small cat" or "a medium-sized dog".

You do not need to use formal language. A short, friendly message is fine.

## How long does your landlord have to reply?

Your landlord has 28 days to write back to you. If they ask for more details about the pet within those 28 days, they get an extra 7 days on top — so up to 35 days in total.

They must reply in writing. If they say yes, great — your pet can move in. If they say no, they must explain their reasons.

## What counts as a good reason to say no?

The law does not set out a fixed list of valid reasons. Landlords can take into account the size of the property, the outdoor space, or the type of animal. What they cannot do is refuse every request without thinking about it. A blanket "no pets" answer is not a valid reason on its own.

## What to do if your landlord says no

If you think the refusal is unfair, you can challenge it in court. Before going to court, get some free advice first. Citizens Advice and Shelter both offer free help to renters and can tell you whether the refusal looks unreasonable.

If you are unsure what to do, try writing a polite second message. Explain that you believe the refusal does not have a good reason behind it, and ask your landlord to reconsider.

## A few things to keep in mind

This right only applies to private rented homes in England. It does not cover social housing — such as homes rented from a housing association or a council.

Remember: this is a right to ask, not an automatic right to have a pet. Your landlord can still say no if they have a genuine reason. But they cannot refuse without one.
