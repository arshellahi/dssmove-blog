No landlord in England can now force you out of your home without a legal reason. The Renters' Rights Act 2026 banned no-fault Section 21 evictions from 1 May 2026.

But if you received a Section 21 notice before that date, you may be wondering where you stand. This post explains your rights and what to do.

## What is a Section 21 notice?

A Section 21 notice was the way private landlords ended a tenancy without giving a reason. They are now banned in England.

Since 1 May 2026, your landlord cannot give you a new Section 21 notice. If they try to, it has no legal force.

## If your landlord served a Section 21 before 1 May 2026

The law allowed a short transition period. If your landlord gave you a Section 21 notice before 1 May 2026, they can still try to use it — but only if they act quickly.

They must start court proceedings by **31 July 2026**. After that date, the Section 21 process closes for good. Your landlord would then need a specific legal reason to seek possession.

If your landlord has not started court proceedings by 31 July 2026, the notice expires. You do not have to leave.

## Is your notice valid?

Not every Section 21 notice is legally valid. Yours may be invalid if:

- it gives you less than 2 months' notice to move out
- your landlord did not protect your deposit in a government-approved scheme
- your landlord did not give you the required documents, such as the How to Rent guide or your energy performance certificate

If your notice is invalid, it has no legal force. You can challenge it. But do not wait — get advice as soon as possible.

## What to do now

**Check your notice.** Citizens Advice has a free online checker to help you work through whether your Section 21 is valid.

**Write down 31 July 2026.** If your landlord has not started court proceedings by that date, the notice expires. You gain the full protections of the Renters' Rights Act.

**Get help early.** If a court date has been set, contact Shelter or Citizens Advice straight away. Both offer free advice and can help you prepare for or attend a hearing.

## What changes after 31 July 2026

Any landlord who wants you to leave after 31 July 2026 must use the Section 8 process. That means proving a specific legal ground — such as rent arrears or anti-social behaviour. A court will then decide.

You will also hold an assured periodic tenancy. Your tenancy rolls on month to month with no end date, your rent can only go up once a year with proper notice, and you have stronger protections against eviction.
