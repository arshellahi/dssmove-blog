If you rent a home privately in England, the law is moving in your favour on dangerous repairs. A new consultation gives you a chance to shape the rules — and legal protections already apply to you.

## What Awaab's Law does

Awaab's Law sets strict deadlines for landlords to investigate and fix dangerous problems in homes.

The law started in October 2025 for social housing — council homes and housing association properties. Under those rules, an emergency hazard — such as a gas leak or a dangerous electrical fault — must be investigated and made safe within 24 hours of you reporting it. Serious but non-emergency hazards must be investigated within 10 working days, with repairs starting within 5 working days of the investigation finishing.

## It already covers your private tenancy

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 put Awaab's Law into private tenancy agreements in England as a legal term. That means your landlord is already legally required to comply with it.

What the government has not yet set are the specific timescales — exactly how many hours or days a private landlord has to act once you report a hazard. The consultation will decide that.

## What the consultation is asking

The government opened a consultation on 9 October 2026. It runs until 18 December 2026.

It is asking for views on:

- Which hazards should be covered — including damp and mould, excess cold, fire risks, electrical faults, and structural problems
- How quickly your landlord must investigate after you report a hazard
- How quickly they must begin repairs after investigating
- Whether the timescales should match social housing or be adjusted for private renting

Officials estimate that once the private sector timescales are in place, the number of homes covered by these repair protections could more than double — from around 4 million to around 9 million homes across England.

## What you can do right now

You do not have to wait for the consultation to finish to ask your landlord to fix something dangerous.

Report the hazard to your landlord in writing — email, letter, or text — and keep a copy with the date visible. If your landlord does not respond or act, Citizens Advice and Shelter can both explain your current rights and the steps available to you, free of charge.

## Having your say

If you rent privately and have had problems getting repairs done, you can submit your views to this consultation through GOV.UK.

Your input will help set the timescales and rules that every private landlord in England will have to follow. This is a chance to help shape protections that will cover millions of homes.
