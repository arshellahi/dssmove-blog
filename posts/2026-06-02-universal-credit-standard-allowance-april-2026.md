From 6 April 2026, the standard allowance in Universal Credit (UC) went up for every claimant. If you are on UC, your payment for any assessment period starting on or after that date should be higher — even if nothing else in your life has changed.

## What is the standard allowance?

The standard allowance is the base amount of UC every claimant receives. It is meant to cover everyday living costs such as food, clothing, and household bills. Any other elements you are entitled to — housing, children, caring, or a health condition — are added on top.

## The new amounts from April 2026

Here are the standard allowance rates that apply from 6 April 2026:

- **Single, aged under 25:** £338.58 a month
- **Single, aged 25 or over:** £424.90 a month
- **Joint claim, both under 25:** £528.34 a month
- **Joint claim, one or both aged 25 or over:** £666.97 a month

The previous rates were £316.98, £400.14, £497.55, and £628.10 respectively. The rise is higher than the rate of inflation, which means your standard allowance should stretch slightly further than it did last year.

## How to check your payment

Log in to your UC account at gov.uk and open your latest statement. Find the line labelled "standard allowance". It should show the new, higher figure for any assessment period that started on or after 6 April 2026.

Your statement also shows the start and end dates of the period it covers, so you can see exactly which rate applies.

## If the amount looks wrong

Mistakes are rare, but if your standard allowance still shows the old lower figure, write a message through your UC journal. Explain what you have noticed and ask DWP to check. If an error is confirmed, DWP should pay back any difference.

If you do not hear back within a reasonable time, or you feel unsure about what to write, Citizens Advice offers free help. They can guide you through the process step by step.

## Your housing costs are separate

Your standard allowance does not pay your rent. If you rent privately, your UC also includes a housing element based on your Local Housing Allowance rate. That figure is calculated separately. If your rent changes or your household size changes, your housing element may change too — but your standard allowance stays at the rates above.
