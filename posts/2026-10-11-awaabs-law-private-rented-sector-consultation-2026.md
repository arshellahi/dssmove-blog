If you rent privately in England, a new government consultation could mean your landlord has to fix dangerous problems within strict time limits — the same rights social housing tenants already have. The consultation runs until 18 December 2026 and you can share your experience.

## What Awaab's Law already does

Awaab's Law gives tenants in social housing in England strict deadlines when something dangerous goes wrong in their home.

Under the current rules:

- **Emergency hazards** — such as dangerous electrical faults or a serious gas leak — must be investigated and made safe within **24 hours**.
- **Significant hazards** like severe damp and mould must be investigated within **10 working days**. Your landlord must then start repair work within **5 working days** of finishing the investigation.

These rights were introduced in October 2025 and are being expanded to more hazard types from 30 November 2026.

## What could change for private renters?

Right now, Awaab's Law covers around **4 million** social rented homes — council properties and housing associations.

The government wants to extend it to private rented homes too. If the consultation leads to new rules, protection could grow to up to **9 million homes** across England.

On 9 October 2026, the government published its consultation asking for views on how the rules should work for private renters. That includes which hazards should be covered and what timescales private landlords should meet.

## Why does this matter?

Private renters can already ask their landlord to fix repairs, but there are no specific legally set deadlines for how quickly they must act.

Bringing in strict timescales could be a real step forward if you live somewhere with damp, mould, or other serious hazards and your landlord is slow to respond.

## How to have your say

The consultation is open from 9 October to **18 December 2026**. You can respond online at gov.uk — you do not need to be an expert. Sharing your experience as a private renter is exactly the kind of information the government says it needs.

Your views could help shape how quickly private landlords must act under any new rules.

## What you can do now

While the consultation runs, your existing rights still apply. If your private landlord is not fixing a problem:

- **Report it in writing** — email or letter, keeping a copy with the date.
- **Take photographs** of the problem and note when you reported it.
- **Get free advice** from Citizens Advice (citizensadvice.org.uk) or Shelter (shelter.org.uk/get-help) on your current rights and next steps.
