## Your right to be considered fairly

If you have ever been turned down for a home because you claim Universal Credit (UC) or Housing Benefit, that experience was wrong — and it is now illegal.

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into force in England on 1 May 2026. Under it, a private landlord or letting agent cannot refuse to rent a home to you because you receive benefits. They also cannot advertise a property in a way that puts off benefits claimants — for example, by saying "no DSS" in a listing.

## What the law now says

The Act makes it unlawful to treat a potential tenant less favourably because they claim benefits. This covers:

- Refusing your application because you get UC or Housing Benefit
- Advertising the property as not suitable for benefit claimants
- Applying stricter financial tests to you than to other applicants

Landlords and agents can still carry out referencing and affordability checks. But they must apply the same checks to every applicant. Your benefits status alone is not a reason they can use to turn you down.

## Rent in advance

The Act also limits how much rent a landlord can ask for before you move in. Once you have signed your tenancy agreement, a landlord can ask for a maximum of 1 month's rent in advance. They cannot ask for any rent before you have signed.

This matters for people on UC and Housing Benefit. Some landlords previously asked for several months upfront because they were worried about benefit payment delays. That is no longer allowed.

## If you think a landlord refused you unfairly

Keep a record of what was said — in writing if possible. Then contact Citizens Advice or Shelter. They can help you work out what happened and whether to make a complaint to your local council.

Councils now have powers to investigate and take action against landlords and agents who break these rules.

## What this means for your housing search

You still need to show you can afford the rent. Your UC housing element or Housing Benefit, plus any other income you have, will form part of any affordability check.

If your Local Housing Allowance (LHA) does not cover the full rent, your council may be able to bridge the gap with a [Discretionary Housing Payment](/apply-for-discretionary-housing-payment/).

The Renters' Rights Act gives you a fairer start when applying for a private let. If you are on benefits and looking for a home in England, you have stronger legal protections now than you did before 1 May 2026.
