PIP helps with the extra costs of being disabled or having a long-term health condition. Many people who rent and claim Universal Credit also receive it. In September 2026, the Timms Review published its first concrete proposals about changing how PIP works.

These are called emerging recommendations. They are not yet law. Here is what they say.

## What the review is proposing

**No routine reviews for lifelong conditions.** People with lifelong or degenerative conditions should not face routine award reviews if no improvement in their condition is expected. At the moment, DWP can schedule a review even when evidence shows nothing will change. This proposal would stop that.

**Better capture of fluctuating conditions.** Some conditions are much worse on some days than others. The review proposes changes to better capture fluctuating conditions. Assessments would consider how your condition affects you across time, not just on the day you are assessed.

**A simpler PIP2 form.** The PIP2 form — where you explain how your condition affects you — would be updated. It would come with clearer guidance on what supporting evidence to include.

**Face-to-face assessments where needed.** Where evidence is limited or unclear, a face-to-face assessment would be available. This would help reach a fair decision for you.

## What these proposals mean for you

The review says PIP should remain a flexible cash payment for the extra costs of disability. These proposals are about making the process fairer, not changing what PIP is.

Workshops across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland are testing the proposals during September and October 2026. The final report is expected later in autumn 2026. It will set out what changes the government is asked to make and when.

Your current PIP award is not affected. Nothing changes until the government responds to the final report.

If you have questions about your existing PIP claim, Citizens Advice can help for free. Shelter is available if housing costs are part of your situation.
