## What is changing?

If you claim Housing Benefit and you live in temporary or supported housing, you may worry that working more hours will cut your housing support. That worry has been real — until now.

From **5 October 2026**, new regulations change how Housing Benefit is calculated for people in these situations. The Housing Benefit (Earned Income Disregards) Regulations 2026 were laid before Parliament on 6 July 2026. They will give around **315,000 people** in England a fairer deal when they earn money.

## How the old rules created a problem

Universal Credit (UC) has a **work allowance**. This means you can earn a set amount each month before your UC starts to reduce. Housing Benefit did not have the same protection.

That created what the government calls a "cliff edge". If you claimed Housing Benefit in supported or temporary housing and you started earning, your benefit could fall sharply — even for a small pay rise. This made it harder to get ahead by working.

## What the new rules do

Five new **earned income disregards** will be added to Housing Benefit. An earned income disregard works like a work allowance. It means some of your earnings are ignored when Housing Benefit is worked out.

This brings Housing Benefit into line with Universal Credit. You will be able to earn more before your housing support is affected.

Nobody will be made worse off by this change.

## Who this affects

The new rules cover working-age people who:

- live in **temporary accommodation** placed by their council, and
- claim Housing Benefit from the council to help pay their rent.

They also cover people in **supported housing** — places like hostels, refuges, or supported flats where you get care or support alongside your housing.

If you live in a private rental and you claim Universal Credit, your housing support already works through the housing cost element of UC. These new disregards are specifically for Housing Benefit claimants in temporary or supported accommodation.

## What you can do now

The rules do not start until **5 October 2026**, so you have time to prepare.

1. **Check your current Housing Benefit.** Call your council or log in to their Housing Benefit portal to see how your earnings affect your award today.
2. **Talk to Citizens Advice.** They can work out exactly how much you could earn from October without losing Housing Benefit.
3. **Plan ahead.** If earning more could help you save for a deposit or rent in advance, start planning now so you are ready when October comes.

Citizens Advice offers free, confidential advice in person, by phone, and online at [citizensadvice.org.uk](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk).
