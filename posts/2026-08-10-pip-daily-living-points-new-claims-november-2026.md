If you have a disability or long-term health condition, Personal Independence Payment (PIP) can pay you a regular weekly amount on top of Universal Credit or other benefits you get. Your income, savings, and employment status do not affect your eligibility.

PIP is split into two parts: daily living and mobility. This post focuses on the daily living part and an important change coming in November 2026.

## How the current scoring works

PIP uses a points system. An assessor looks at how your condition affects you across ten daily living activities — things like preparing food, washing and dressing, managing medication, and communicating with other people.

Each activity has a list of descriptors. Each descriptor describes how much difficulty you have with the task and carries a set number of points. You receive the highest-scoring descriptor that fits your situation.

You need at least 8 points in total to receive the standard daily living rate. You need at least 12 points for the enhanced rate.

Right now you can combine points from several activities to reach that total. Scoring 3 points in one activity and 5 in another, for example, adds up to 8 and could qualify you for the standard rate.

## What changes for new claims from November 2026

From November 2026, there is an extra rule for new PIP claims. As well as reaching 8 or 12 points overall, you will also need to score at least 4 points in a single daily living activity.

For example, scoring 2 points each in four different activities adds up to 8 in total — but under the new rule that would not be enough, because no single activity reached 4 points.

This change applies only to new claims made on or after November 2026.

## If you already get PIP

If you are already receiving PIP, the new threshold does not apply to you yet. Your current award continues under the existing rules. The new rule will only be considered when your award next comes up for a scheduled review.

## What to do if you have not yet claimed

If you have a health condition or disability that makes daily tasks harder and you have not claimed PIP, it is worth checking the activities list on GOV.UK to see whether your situation is likely to score points.

Citizens Advice can help you check your eligibility, support you through the form, and prepare you for the assessment — all free of charge. Turn2us also has a benefits calculator that can show you what you could be entitled to.
