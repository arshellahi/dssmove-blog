If you claim Universal Credit (UC) and rent privately, you have something useful to factor into your budget for April 2027.

The Universal Credit Act 2025 locks in a guarantee: the standard allowance — the base part of your UC payment — must rise by more than inflation every April until 2030.

## What the standard allowance is

The standard allowance is the core amount every UC claimant gets before any extras for housing costs, children, or disability.

In 2026 to 2027 it is:

- Single person aged 25 or over: £424.90 a month
- Single person under 25: £338.58 a month

## What above inflation means

Most benefits rise in line with the Consumer Prices Index (CPI) inflation figure each April. The Universal Credit Act 2025 adds an extra rise on top of CPI for the standard allowance:

- April 2026: 2.3% above CPI — already in your payment
- April 2027: 3.1% above CPI
- April 2028: 4.0% above CPI
- April 2029: 4.8% above CPI

By 2030, your standard allowance will be about £21 a month — or around £250 a year — more than it would have been if it had only risen with inflation.

The exact April 2027 cash amount will depend on the September 2026 CPI figure. DWP publishes the new rates in early 2027.

## Why this matters for your rent

If your landlord raises your rent, knowing your standard allowance is set to grow faster than prices gives you a bit more breathing room in your budget.

It does not solve everything. The housing cost element of UC — which covers rent directly — rises with inflation only, not above it. Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rates, which cap how much UC will contribute to your rent, are also set separately.

If your rent is already above your LHA rate, the standard allowance rise gives you a little extra help bridging that gap.

## What to do right now

If you are already struggling with a shortfall between your rent and your LHA rate, do not wait until April 2027 to ask for help.

Contact your council and ask about a Discretionary Housing Payment. This is a short-term payment that can cover the difference between your rent and your LHA rate while you look for more affordable housing.

Citizens Advice can also do a free check of your full UC entitlement. You may be entitled to elements you are not currently claiming.
