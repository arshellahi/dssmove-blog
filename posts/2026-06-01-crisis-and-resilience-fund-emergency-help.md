If you are on Universal Credit (UC) or Housing Benefit and struggling to cover food, heating, or other essentials, your local council may now be able to help.

A new fund called the Crisis and Resilience Fund (CRF) started in April 2026. It replaced the old Household Support Fund, which ended in March 2026. The CRF runs until March 2029. Help is available right now.

## What the fund can help with

The CRF is for people on low incomes who face a sudden financial shock. Councils can use their share of the fund in different ways. Common types of help include:

- food vouchers or referrals to a food bank
- help paying energy bills or topping up a prepayment meter
- support with essential household items such as a cooker or washing machine
- other one-off payments for urgent costs

Your council decides exactly what it can offer. Contact them to find out what is available in your area.

## Who can apply

You do not need to be on UC or Housing Benefit to apply. But being on a means-tested benefit is a good way to show financial need. Your council will look at your income, your savings, and your situation when making a decision.

There is no fixed payment amount. Some councils give more than others depending on how much funding they received and how many people they are helping.

If your main worry is rent — for example, your rent is higher than your Local Housing Allowance rate — a [Discretionary Housing Payment](/apply-for-discretionary-housing-payment/) may be a better fit for your situation.

## How to apply

There is no national application form. Each council runs its own process.

1. Search for your council name and "Crisis and Resilience Fund" online.
2. Find the application form, phone number, or contact page.
3. Be ready to explain your situation and show proof of income. A UC award letter or a recent bank statement will usually do.

If you are not sure where to start, Citizens Advice can help you find the right person to speak to.

## If your council says no

Ask the council to review the decision. The CRF is discretionary, which means each case is looked at on its own. Citizens Advice can help you put together a review request.

It is also worth checking whether you qualify for other support — such as a council tax reduction. Citizens Advice has a free benefits check tool that can show you what else you might be entitled to.
