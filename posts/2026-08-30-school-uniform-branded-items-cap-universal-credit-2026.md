## What changes from 1 September 2026

From tomorrow, every school in England must follow a new legal limit on branded school uniforms.

The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 says schools can only ask you to buy a certain number of branded items.

At primary school, the limit is **3 branded items**. At secondary school, the limit is **4 branded items** — but only if one of those items is a tie. If the school does not require a tie, the limit stays at 3.

If your child's school asks for more branded items than this, it is breaking the law.

## What counts as a branded item

A school uniform item is "branded" if it has the school name or logo on it, or if it can only be bought from a particular shop because of its colour, design, or style.

This matters because branded items usually cost more than plain alternatives from supermarkets. Schools often sell branded items through a single approved supplier, which removes your ability to shop around and pushes prices up.

The cap covers:

- Branded jumpers, sweatshirts, and polo shirts
- Branded PE kit
- Branded school bags and book bags

Ties count towards the secondary school limit. Non-branded items — like plain white shirts, black trousers, or black shoes — are not counted. Schools can still require those.

## How to save on the rest of the uniform

For non-branded items, you can shop anywhere you like. Supermarkets typically sell plain school trousers, skirts, shirts, and shoes at low prices. Buying in August or September usually gives you the best selection.

Second-hand uniform sales are also worth checking — many schools and parent groups run these for free or at low cost.

## Getting help from your council if you claim Universal Credit

Many councils in England offer a school clothing grant to help families on low incomes buy uniform. If you claim Universal Credit, you may qualify.

Grant amounts and eligibility vary by council. Here is how to find out:

1. **Search online for "[your council name] school clothing grant 2026"** or call your council's main benefits number.
2. **Apply quickly** — some councils stop taking new applications before the school term starts.
3. **Have your National Insurance number ready** — councils usually check your Universal Credit status to confirm eligibility.

If your council does not run a grant, ask whether any other back-to-school support is available, such as supermarket vouchers or emergency help funds.

## If your school asks for too many branded items

Schools have known about this law since it received Royal Assent in April 2026. If your child's school is still asking for more branded items than the limit allows, you do not have to buy them.

Write to the school asking it to confirm its uniform policy complies with the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026. If the school does not update its policy, your local council's school admissions or education welfare team can advise you on next steps.

Citizens Advice can also help you understand your rights if you need support challenging the school's approach.
