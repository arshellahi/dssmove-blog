If you are struggling with rent, food or energy bills, your local council may be able to give you emergency help through the **Crisis and Resilience Fund (CRF)**.

The fund started on 1 April 2026 and replaces the previous Household Support Fund. It runs until March 2029, which means councils have more certainty to plan support over a longer period.

## What the fund can help with

The CRF is for people on a low income who face a sudden financial shock. Your council can use it to help with:

- food and essential household items
- energy, water and heating bills
- rent shortfalls or moving costs
- essential furniture or appliances
- emergency travel costs

Each council runs its own scheme, so the exact support available near you will differ. Check your council's website for the local rules.

## Help with rent

If you are on Universal Credit or Housing Benefit and cannot meet your full rent, you can apply for a **Housing Payment** through your council. This is short-term support for a rent shortfall or costs linked to moving home — for example, a deposit you cannot cover.

This replaces the support that used to be called a Discretionary Housing Payment. If you have applied for one of those before, the process at your council will be similar. Contact your council's housing or benefits team to make a fresh application.

## How to apply

Search for your council's name plus "Crisis and Resilience Fund" to find their local page. Some councils also let you apply through your local Citizens Advice service.

When you apply, your council will ask about your income, outgoings and what you need help with. Being specific about your situation will help your application.

You do not need to already be in debt to apply. Most councils prioritise households on a low income who have had an unexpected cost, and the fund is meant to help you before things get worse.

## Get support with your application

You do not have to apply on your own. [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/) can help you understand your options and fill in the form for free. Visit their website or contact your local branch.

You can also speak directly to your council's welfare team. They can tell you which types of support are available in your area and what evidence to bring to your application.
