## More freedom to try work

If you claim Universal Credit (UC) and have a health condition or disability, you may have worried about trying work. You might fear that a few hours a week could trigger DWP to check your payments again. That fear has now been addressed in law.

From 30 April 2026, new rules called the Right to Try mean that starting paid work or volunteering will not, by itself, cause DWP to reassess your UC health element or your Personal Independence Payment (PIP).

This applies once DWP has already assessed you and found you have Limited Capability for Work (LCW) or Limited Capability for Work-Related Activity (LCWRA).

## What the Right to Try covers

Under the new rules:

- Starting a paid job or trial will not trigger a Work Capability Assessment (WCA).
- Doing voluntary work will not trigger a WCA.
- Taking on paid or voluntary work will not, by itself, lead to a fresh review of your PIP award.

This gives you more space to test what you can manage. You can try a few hours, see how your health holds up, and step back if you need to — without worrying that a reassessment is on its way just because you tried.

## What it does not cover

The Right to Try is not a complete shield. DWP can still reassess you if your health condition improves significantly, if there is a clear change in your ability to work, or if there is a concern about fraud.

The rule only applies once DWP has assessed you. If you are still waiting for your first Work Capability Assessment, the new protection does not apply yet.

It also does not remove any limits on how much you can earn while claiming your current benefits. Check with Citizens Advice if you want to know exactly where those limits sit for your situation.

## Getting advice before you start

Before you begin any job or volunteering, talk to Citizens Advice. They can check how the Right to Try applies to your individual payments and help you keep notes about your health and activity. If DWP ever asks questions, good records help.

Shelter can also support you if you are worried your tenancy or rent support might be affected. If you earn more, the housing element of your UC may adjust — knowing this before you start helps you plan ahead.
