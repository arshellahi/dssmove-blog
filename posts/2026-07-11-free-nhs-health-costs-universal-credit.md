Claiming Universal Credit can do more than help with your rent. If you live in England, it may also mean you pay nothing for NHS prescriptions, dental check-ups, and sight tests. Many people on UC never claim these savings because they do not know they exist.

## What you can get for free

If you qualify, you can get:

- free NHS prescriptions
- free NHS dental treatment
- free NHS eye tests
- help with the cost of glasses or contact lenses

Each prescription in England costs £9.90. If you or someone in your household takes regular medication, getting prescriptions free can save a significant amount each year.

## Who qualifies

You qualify automatically if you claim Universal Credit and have no earnings from work. Your UC statement will show your take-home pay for the assessment period. If it reads £0.00, you qualify.

If you do some work, you may still qualify. You are entitled to free NHS health costs if your Universal Credit includes a child element or a Limited Capability for Work element, and your take-home pay in the most recent assessment period was £935 or less. This limit applies to the joint earnings of you and your partner if you claim as a couple.

## How to prove you qualify

There is no automatic card sent to you. You need to tell your pharmacist, dentist, or optician that you claim Universal Credit. If asked for proof, you can show your UC award letter or a screenshot of your online account.

If you pay for a prescription by mistake, ask the pharmacy for form FP57 at the time of purchase. You have up to 3 months to claim a refund.

## If your earnings are above the limit

You may still get help through the NHS Low Income Scheme. Fill in form HC1, available free from GOV.UK or your local Jobcentre Plus. If you qualify, you get an HC2 certificate that covers prescriptions, dental treatment, sight tests, and sometimes travel costs for health appointments.

## Prescription prepayment certificates

If you do not qualify for free prescriptions but need several items each month, a prescription prepayment certificate (PPC) may save you money. A PPC lets you collect as many NHS prescriptions as you need for a fixed quarterly or annual fee. The government froze both PPC rates for 2026 to 2027. Check GOV.UK for the current prices.

For a full breakdown of what you may qualify for, visit GOV.UK and search for "help with NHS costs". Citizens Advice also has a step-by-step guide if you need help working out whether you qualify.
