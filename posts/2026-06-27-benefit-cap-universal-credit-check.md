The benefit cap is a limit on the total benefits most households can receive each month. If your total household benefits go over the cap, your Universal Credit (UC) is reduced. The cut usually comes out of your housing element. That can mean you are short of rent.

## What are the cap limits?

From April 2026, the limits are:

**Outside Greater London**

- £423.46 a week — couples and lone parents with children
- £283.71 a week — single people with no children

**Greater London**

- £2,110.25 a month — couples and lone parents with children
- £1,413.92 a month — single people with no children

## Am I exempt?

Many people are not affected by the cap at all. The cap does not apply to you if:

- **You get the LCWRA element in your UC.** This is the extra payment if DWP decides your health condition means you cannot prepare for work. If you have LCWRA, the cap does not apply.

- **You get the carer element in your UC.** You can get this if you care for a disabled person for at least 35 hours a week. They must receive PIP, DLA, or a similar qualifying disability benefit.

- **You earn enough.** If you and your partner earn at least £881 a month combined, the cap does not apply. This threshold went up from £846 on 1 April 2026.

- **You or your partner get Pension Credit.** If so, you are not affected.

## How to spot the cap in your UC account

Log in to your UC online account. Look for a line called **benefit cap deduction** in your statement. If it is there, that is the amount being removed each month.

If you have never seen this line, the cap is probably not affecting you. But check again whenever your income or family situation changes.

## What to do if the cap is affecting you

**Talk to Citizens Advice.** They can check whether you should be getting an exemption. For example, if you have a health condition that has not yet been assessed, you might qualify for LCWRA — and that would lift the cap completely.

**Apply for a Discretionary Housing Payment.** Your council can make a short-term payment to help cover the rent gap. You have to apply — it is not automatic. Our guide on [Discretionary Housing Payments](/apply-for-discretionary-housing-payment/) explains what to expect and how to apply.

**Check your earnings.** If you or your partner can earn £881 a month between you, the cap stops. Even some part-time work could make the difference.

If the cap is putting your tenancy at risk, contact [Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk) for free housing advice.
