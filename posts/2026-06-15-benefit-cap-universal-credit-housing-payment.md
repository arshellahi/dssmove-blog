Thousands of households use Universal Credit (UC) to help pay private rent. But there is a rule called the benefit cap that can quietly reduce the housing element of your UC. If your payment seems lower than it should be, the cap is worth checking.

## What is the benefit cap?

The benefit cap sets a limit on the total amount of UC you can get each month. If your UC — adding together the housing element, your standard allowance, and any child payments — would go above that limit, DWP cuts the housing element to bring the total back under it.

This is why some renters end up getting less toward their rent than the Local Housing Allowance rate for their area, even when their rent is within that rate.

## Who is exempt from the cap?

The benefit cap does not apply to you if:

- you or your partner **earn £881 or more a month** after tax and National Insurance
- you receive the **LCWRA element** on UC — meaning a health condition limits your ability to work
- you have a **carer's element** on UC because you look after a disabled person
- you or your partner receive the **PIP daily living component**, Disability Living Allowance (DLA), or the Armed Forces Independence Payment

If you recently stopped earning £881 a month — after working steadily for 12 months or more — DWP can give you a **9-month grace period** before the cap applies. When you first claim UC, tell DWP about your previous earnings so they can check.

## How to see if the cap is affecting you

Log in to your UC online account and open your latest payment breakdown. Look for a line called **"benefit cap deduction"**. That figure is the amount being taken off your housing element each month.

Not sure how to read your statement? Call the Universal Credit helpline free on 0800 328 5644 or speak to a [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/) adviser — both options are free.

## What to do if you are capped

**Report a change of circumstances.** If you have started working, received an LCWRA decision, or taken on a caring role since you claimed, tell DWP straight away. An exemption can take effect from your next assessment period.

**Apply for a Crisis and Resilience Fund Housing Payment.** If you still have a rent shortfall, contact your local council. This government-funded grant can top up your rent for several months while your situation is resolved. Search "[your council name] Crisis and Resilience Fund" to find the form for your area.

**Get free help.** [Shelter](https://england.shelter.org.uk/) and Citizens Advice can check that DWP has applied the cap correctly and help you challenge it if they have made an error.
