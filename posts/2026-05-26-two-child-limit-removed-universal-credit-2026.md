## What the two-child limit was

Universal Credit (UC) used to pay a child element only for your first 2 children. If you had a third or subsequent child born on or after 6 April 2017, you got nothing extra for them in UC.

That rule — called the two-child limit — is now gone.

## What changed on 6 April 2026

From 6 April 2026, UC pays a child element for every child you have. There is no longer a cap at 2.

The monthly rate depends on when your eldest child was born.

If your eldest child was born before 6 April 2017, UC pays **£351.88 a month** for that child. For every other child in your household, the rate is **£303.94 a month**.

If all your children were born on or after 6 April 2017, UC pays **£303.94 a month** for each of them.

A family whose third child was previously excluded could now receive an extra £303.94 each month. Over a full year, that adds up to around £3,650.

## How this can help you as a renter

Your child element forms part of your total monthly UC payment. You can spend it on any essential cost, including rent.

Many private renters on UC find their housing element does not fully cover the rent. Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rates were frozen from April 2024, while private rents have risen in many areas since then. Extra income from a new child element can help close that gap.

It can also make a difference during affordability checks. Some landlords want to see that your monthly income meets a set level before they agree to rent to you. A higher UC payment — now that it includes every child — makes that easier to show.

## What to do right now

You should not need to make a new UC claim. DWP will update your payment automatically for any child who was previously left out.

To make sure it has happened, log in to your **UC online journal**. Look at your payment breakdown and check that a child element appears for each child. If a child is missing, report it through the journal message system.

If your payment has not changed and you believe it should have, do not wait. Get free help from **[Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk)** — they can check your entitlement and help you chase DWP if there has been a mistake.
