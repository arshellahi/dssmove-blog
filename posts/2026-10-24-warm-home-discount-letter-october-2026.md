The government started posting £150 Warm Home Discount letters from 22 October 2026. If you claim Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, Pension Credit, or income-related ESA, look out for a letter from your energy supplier in the coming days.

The letter simply confirms that the £150 will be taken off your electricity bill this winter. You do not need to do anything with it — the money comes off automatically.

## What the £150 discount means for you

The Warm Home Discount takes £150 straight off your electricity account. You do not receive cash. You do not repay it.

If you pay for electricity through a prepayment meter, the £150 is added as credit instead. Payments for most eligible households will be made by the end of March 2027.

## Why you might not receive a letter

The government matches benefit records to electricity accounts automatically. If the details do not line up, a match can fail — even if you qualify. Common reasons include:

- The electricity account is in a partner's name or a landlord's name
- You moved home after August 2026 and your address records have not been updated
- Your energy supplier does not take part in the scheme

If you have not received a letter by the end of November, do not assume you are not eligible.

## What to do if no letter arrives

1. Call your electricity supplier and ask about the Warm Home Discount. Have your name, address, date of birth, and the benefit you receive ready.
2. If your supplier says you are not matched, ask them to check whether your account details and benefit records share the same address.
3. If your supplier does not take part in the scheme, Citizens Advice can help you find one that does and check what your switching options are.

There is also a separate route for households on a low income who were not on one of the qualifying benefits on 23 August 2026. Ask your energy supplier about this directly.

## If you moved home or changed supplier recently

If you moved home or switched energy supplier after August 2026, your letter may have been sent to the wrong address. Contact your new supplier and let them know which benefit you receive so they can check your record.

## Keep your benefit details up to date

The government matches your benefit record to your electricity account. If your Universal Credit claim still shows an old address, the match can fail. Log in to your Universal Credit online account and check your address is current. Your energy account needs to show the same address.

If you are not sure whether you qualify, Citizens Advice offers a free benefits check and can help you contact your energy supplier.
