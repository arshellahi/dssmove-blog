If you rent your home and claim Universal Credit (UC), there is a rule that may be quietly cutting your rent help each month.

It is called a non-dependant deduction. The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) assumes that any other adult normally living in your home should help with the rent. So DWP takes money off your housing costs element to reflect that.

## How much is taken off?

In 2026/27, DWP deducts £96.55 a month for each non-dependant adult who normally lives with you. That adds up to more than £1,150 a year per person.

A non-dependant is typically a grown-up son or daughter, a sibling, a parent, or any other adult who lives in your home but is not your partner and not a dependent child.

## How to check your UC statement

Log in to your UC online account and open your most recent payment breakdown. Look for "housing cost contribution" in the housing costs section. If you see a figure there, that is a non-dependant deduction.

If the amount surprises you, check whether the person DWP has recorded actually does normally live in your home full-time.

## When there is no deduction

You will not face any deduction at all if you or your partner currently receive:

- the daily living component of Personal Independence Payment (PIP)
- the care component of Disability Living Allowance (DLA) at the middle or higher rate

If either of those applies to you, DWP cannot make a non-dependant deduction from your housing element — no matter who lives with you.

There are also cases where a specific person in your home may be exempt from the deduction. For example, someone who is in prison, someone who normally lives elsewhere, or someone who gets Pension Credit or Carer's Allowance will generally not trigger a deduction.

## When you must update DWP

If the adult moves out, report it in your UC journal as soon as it happens. DWP will not remove the deduction automatically. Any extra UC you receive because you did not report the move could be recovered from you later as an overpayment.

Equally, if a new adult moves in — for example, a child returns home after university — you need to report that change too.

## Getting help

If you are unsure whether a deduction should apply to you, contact Citizens Advice or Shelter for a free check. They can look at your full UC award, confirm whether the deduction is correct, and help you ask DWP to change it if it is not.
