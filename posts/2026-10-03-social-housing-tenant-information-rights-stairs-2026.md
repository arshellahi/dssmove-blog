If you rent from a housing association in England, something important changed this week.

From 1 October 2026, housing associations must start publishing key information about how they manage your home. The rules are called the Social Tenant Access to Information Requirements — STAIRs for short.

## What your housing association must now publish

The new rules arrive in two phases.

**Phase 1 started on 1 October 2026.** From now on, housing associations must proactively publish information in seven areas:

- **Who runs things** — staff names and roles, organisational structure, and how tenants are consulted on decisions
- **Spending** — how grants and service charge money are used
- **Your building** — maintenance plans, repairs schedules, and any plans to transfer the housing stock
- **Performance** — satisfaction scores from tenants, complaint figures, how quickly repairs are done
- **Services** — what services are on offer and how to get help
- **Registers** — legally required lists, such as relevant planning permissions
- **Management policies** — how they manage tenancies, including eviction policies

You should be able to find all of this on your housing association's website from now on. Look for a section called a publication scheme or an information scheme.

**Phase 2 starts in April 2027.** From April, you will be able to formally request specific information that is not already published. This is a new right — similar to the Freedom of Information Act that gives council tenants access to information about their homes.

## Why this matters for you

Before STAIRs, housing associations had no legal duty to share this data with tenants unless they chose to. Now they do.

If your repairs are being ignored, you can check how your housing association rates on repair performance compared to its own promises. If you are unsure how your service charges are calculated, the spending data must now be available. If you have had a complaint handled badly, the complaint figures are on public record.

## What you can do right now

Check your housing association's website for their new publication scheme. If you cannot find it, email them and ask where to find the information published under the Social Tenant Access to Information Requirements.

If you have an urgent problem — such as damp, mould, or a safety issue — do not wait for data. Get help today:

- **Citizens Advice** gives free advice on housing repairs and benefit problems.
- **The Housing Ombudsman** handles complaints about housing associations at no cost.
- **Shelter** can help if your housing problem is affecting your ability to stay in your home.

## Can someone help you make a request?

Yes. When Phase 2 starts in April 2027, you can nominate a representative — such as a Citizens Advice adviser or a local councillor — to make an information request on your behalf. You just need to let your housing association know in writing who that person is.
