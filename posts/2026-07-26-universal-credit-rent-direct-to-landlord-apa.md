If paying rent is hard to manage month to month, there is a simple option that can help. You can ask for the housing part of your Universal Credit (UC) to be paid directly to your landlord.

The money does not pass through your hands first. It goes straight from DWP to your landlord, so your rent is covered and you keep the rest of your UC for other costs.

## What is a managed payment to your landlord?

This is called an **Alternative Payment Arrangement (APA)**, or a **Managed Payment to Landlord**. It does not change how much UC you get — it only changes where the housing costs element is sent.

It is a voluntary option. You choose to set it up, and you can ask for it to be stopped if your situation changes.

## You do not need to be in arrears to ask

Many people assume you can only get direct payments if you are already behind on rent. That is not true. **You can ask for this at any time** — even before any arrears build up.

To request it, contact your work coach or case manager. You can send a message through your UC online journal, or raise it at your next appointment.

## When can your landlord make the request?

Your landlord can also apply for direct payment — but only if you owe rent equal to **2 months or more**. If your landlord makes this request, DWP will send you a message through your UC online journal asking you to respond.

You have the right to object. You can tell DWP you disagree with the request and provide evidence — such as a record of your rent payments — to support your case. DWP will then look at what is in your best interests before making a decision.

## Can you ask for payments twice a month?

Yes. If one large payment each month makes it hard to stay on top of rent and bills, you can ask for your UC to be paid **twice a month** instead of once. Ask your work coach about this, either through your journal or at your next appointment.

## How to set this up

- Tell your work coach you want an Alternative Payment Arrangement for your housing costs
- You can do this by sending a message in your UC online journal
- DWP will then contact your landlord to confirm they can receive direct payments

You can also point your landlord to the Apply for a Direct Rent Payment service on GOV.UK, where they can make a request on your behalf.

If you are not sure whether this is right for you, [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk) can walk you through your options and help you decide.
