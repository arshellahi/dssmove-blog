## What changed in May 2026

Before May 2026, many private tenancies had a fixed end date. When the date arrived, you either signed a new contract or moved out.

From 1 May 2026, the Renters' Rights Act removed fixed end dates from private tenancies in England. All private tenancies are now periodic — they roll on with no end date. You can stay as long as you like. And when you want to leave, you can — you just need to give the right notice.

## How much notice you must give

To end your periodic tenancy, you must give your landlord at least **2 months' written notice**.

Your notice must expire on the first or last day of a tenancy period. A tenancy period is the gap between your regular rent payment dates. If you pay rent on the 1st of each month, your notice must end on the last day of a month or on the 1st.

Give your notice early so it has time to land on a valid end date.

**Example:** Your rent is due on the 1st. You want to leave by 31 October. You need 2 months' notice ending on 31 October. So you must give notice no later than 1 September.

## What counts as written notice

A letter or email is fine. It does not need to be formal or legal.

State clearly that you are ending your tenancy and the date you plan to leave. Keep a copy of what you sent and note when you sent it. That gives you a record if any disagreement arises later.

## Can you agree a shorter notice period?

Yes, but both you and your landlord must agree in writing. You cannot give less than 4 weeks' notice even with agreement.

If your landlord agrees to let you leave sooner, put the agreement in writing and keep a copy. It protects you if there is a later dispute about when your tenancy ended.

## What happens to your rent during notice

You must keep paying rent until the last day of your notice period, even if you move out earlier.

If you stop paying rent before the notice ends, your landlord can pursue you for the unpaid amount. Either stay in the property until the last day or reach a written agreement with your landlord to leave sooner.

## Steps to leaving smoothly

1. **Give written notice.** Email is fine — keep a copy dated on the day you sent it.
2. **Check your notice end date.** Count 2 months forward and land it on the first or last day of a tenancy period.
3. **Arrange a check-out walk-through.** Ask your landlord to inspect the property together before you hand over the keys. Take photos throughout.
4. **Report your move to Universal Credit.** Log in to your account and update your address and housing costs as soon as you move to your new home.
5. **If in doubt, contact Citizens Advice or Shelter.** They offer free advice on notice periods, deposits, and your rights when you move.
