When you pay a deposit to move into a rented home, that money is protected by law. Your landlord must place it in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it.

## What is a tenancy deposit protection scheme?

There are 3 official schemes your landlord can use:

- **Deposit Protection Service (DPS)**
- **MyDeposits**
- **Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS)**

Each scheme keeps your money safe while you rent the property. When you leave, the scheme can handle any dispute about what you are owed — so you get a fair hearing.

Your landlord must also send you written details — called "prescribed information" — within 30 days. This tells you which scheme your deposit is in and explains your rights.

## How to check your deposit is registered

Go to each scheme's website and enter your name and postcode. The lookup is free and takes just a few minutes.

Search all 3 schemes. If you find nothing, your deposit may not be registered. Do not wait too long to check — the 30-day window starts from the day you hand over the money.

## How much can a landlord ask for?

The maximum deposit is **5 weeks' rent** if your annual rent is under £50,000. Most renters in England fall well under this limit.

For example: if you pay £800 a month, your year's rent is £9,600. The most your landlord can ask for is 5 weeks of that — around £923.

If your landlord asked for more than this, you may be entitled to money back. Citizens Advice can help you work out whether that applies to you.

## What if it is not in a scheme?

If your landlord has not protected your deposit within the 30 days, you may be entitled to compensation of up to 3 times the deposit amount, on top of getting your deposit back.

The best first step is to contact **Citizens Advice**. They offer free guidance and can help you decide what to do. **Shelter** also has a free helpline and step-by-step online guides if your situation is more complex.

## Changes since May 2026

From 1 May 2026, most private tenancies in England became assured tenancies under the Renters' Rights Act. The 30-day deposit protection rule has not changed — it still applies to every private tenant in England.

What has changed is that you now have stronger protection against eviction. Your landlord needs a legal reason to ask you to leave, and cannot simply give you a notice to go without one.
