From 6 April 2026, the two-child limit on Universal Credit was removed. You can now receive a child element for every child in your household — not just the first two.

If you have three or more children and you claim Universal Credit, your monthly payment should now be higher. For most families, the change happens automatically.

## What changed

Universal Credit pays a "child element" — an extra amount added to your monthly payment for each child you are responsible for. Before 6 April 2026, this only applied to your first two children. If you had a third or fourth child, you received nothing extra for them.

That rule has now ended. From 6 April 2026, you receive a child element for every child.

## How much is the child element?

In 2026/27, the child element is worth:

- **£351.88 a month** for your oldest child, if they were born before 6 April 2017
- **£303.94 a month** for each other child

A third child adds around £304 a month to your Universal Credit. That works out to about £3,650 extra each year.

If you have a third and a fourth child, your payment could rise by over £600 a month.

## Do you need to do anything?

No. If you already claim Universal Credit, the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) updates your award automatically. You do not need to call anyone, report a change, or fill in extra forms.

Your new payment amount will appear in your Universal Credit journal. Most families see the change in their May or June 2026 payment, depending on when your assessment period starts.

If you are not yet on Universal Credit and you have three or more children, you may be able to make a new claim. Use the Turn2us benefits calculator to check what you could get before you apply.

## What about Child Tax Credit?

Child Tax Credit is being phased out. If you are still on it, DWP will write to you about moving to Universal Credit. The two-child limit will not apply when you move.

If anything is unclear, Citizens Advice can look at your situation for free and help you understand what you are entitled to.
