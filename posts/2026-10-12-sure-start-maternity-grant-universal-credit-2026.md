If you are pregnant or have recently had a baby and you claim Universal Credit (UC), you may be entitled to a free £500 payment from the government.

It is called the Sure Start Maternity Grant. It comes from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) and is not a loan — you never pay it back.

## Who can claim?

You can apply if you or your partner receives one of these benefits:

- Universal Credit (UC)
- Income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)
- Income Support
- Pension Credit

You also need to have no other children under 16 living with you. For most families this means the grant is only available for your first baby.

If you are expecting twins or triplets and you already have one child at home, you may still be able to claim. Citizens Advice can check whether your situation qualifies.

If you have applied for one of the benefits listed above but your claim has not been decided yet, you can still submit your grant form now. DWP will process it once your benefit is confirmed.

## How much is the grant?

The Sure Start Maternity Grant pays **£500**. It goes straight into your bank account, or into the account of the person named on the form. It is paid as a lump sum — you receive it all at once.

## When to apply

You can apply from **11 weeks before** the week your baby is due. The deadline is **6 months after** the birth.

Do not wait until the baby arrives. You can apply in advance of the birth using a form signed by your midwife, and the money can reach you before your due date.

## How to apply

You apply by post using the **SF100 form**. You can download it from GOV.UK, or ask at your nearest Jobcentre Plus.

Part of the form must be completed and signed by a health professional — usually your midwife or GP. They confirm your expected due date or the baby's date of birth.

Once the form is ready, post everything to: **Freepost DWP SSMG**. You do not need a stamp.

If you need help completing the form, Citizens Advice can help you for free. Your Jobcentre Plus work coach can also point you in the right direction.

## What to do if your claim is turned down

If DWP refuses your application, you can ask for a mandatory reconsideration — a free review of the decision. Citizens Advice can help you request this and explain what evidence to include.

## Where to get help

- **Citizens Advice** — free support with eligibility checks and completing the form. Find your nearest branch at citizensadvice.org.uk.
- **Shelter** — if housing costs are adding financial pressure during your pregnancy, Shelter runs a free housing advice helpline at shelter.org.uk.
