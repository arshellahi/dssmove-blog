If your home feels cold in winter or your energy bills are eating into your budget, there may be a way to get it improved for free. The Warm Homes Local Grant is a government scheme that funds energy efficiency upgrades in private rented homes — and if you qualify, you pay nothing at all.

The scheme runs until March 2028. Your local council delivers it, and many areas across England still have money to spend for 2026 to 2027.

## Do you qualify?

To be eligible as a private renter, two main conditions apply:

- Your household income is £36,000 a year or less. This covers all adults living in the home who are not in full-time education.
- Your home has an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating of D, E, F, or G.

You do not need to own the property. You do need your landlord to agree before you can apply.

If you do not know your EPC rating, you can look it up for free using the EPC register on GOV.UK.

## What you could get

The grant can pay for energy efficiency work worth up to £30,000 per home. Improvements can include:

- loft, wall, and underfloor insulation
- solar panels (solar PV)
- double or triple glazing
- heating controls and draught-proofing

Your landlord pays nothing for the first property they improve through the scheme. You pay nothing regardless.

Better insulation means lower bills — not just this winter but every winter after that.

## How to apply

Start at the Warm Homes Local Grant page on GOV.UK (gov.uk/apply-warm-homes-local-grant). It links you to your local council's scheme.

Once you apply, the council will usually contact you within 10 working days to check your details and arrange a free home survey. If the work is approved, a local installer carries it out.

You will need your landlord's permission before you submit your application, so it is worth having that conversation early.

## If your area is already full

Some councils have spent their 2026 to 2027 budget. If yours has, you can usually register your interest for 2027 to 2028 — the scheme runs until March 2028, so it is worth joining the list.

If you would like help talking to your landlord or navigating the application, Citizens Advice and Shelter both offer free support.
