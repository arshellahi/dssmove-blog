## Your right to tenancy information in writing

Since 1 May 2026, the Renters' Rights Act 2025 gives every private renter in England the right to have their key tenancy terms confirmed in writing. This right applies whether you are just moving in or have been renting for years.

Getting this information in writing means you know exactly what you agreed to. It gives you a clear record of your rent, your landlord's details, and your rights — all in one place.

## What new tenants should receive

If you have started or are starting a private tenancy on or after 1 May 2026, your landlord must give you a **written statement of terms** before or at the point you sign. They can include this in a full tenancy agreement or as a separate document.

The written statement must include:

- your landlord's name and an address where you can write to them
- your full name as the tenant
- the address of the home you are renting
- the date your tenancy begins
- your rent amount and how often you pay it (your rent period must not be longer than one month)

Your landlord cannot ask you to sign before you have seen this information.

## What if you were already renting before May 2026?

If you had a written tenancy agreement already in place, your landlord should have given you the official **Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet** by 31 May 2026. This one-page document explains how the law has changed and what it means for your tenancy.

If your tenancy was based on a verbal agreement only — nothing in writing — your landlord should have provided a written statement of your key terms by 31 May 2026.

## Why having it in writing helps you

If you claim **Universal Credit (UC)** or **Housing Benefit**, your written statement makes it easier to report your housing costs correctly. When you start a claim or update an existing one, DWP will ask for your monthly rent and your landlord's details. Your written statement has both.

It also protects you if there is ever a dispute. If your landlord tries to change your rent or other terms, you have written evidence of what was originally agreed.

If you want to challenge a rent increase in the future, you will need to show what your original rent was — your written statement is the record to keep safe.

## What to do right now

If you moved in recently and did not receive any written tenancy information, ask your landlord in writing — by email or text — to send you a copy. Keep a record of your request.

If your landlord does not reply or refuses, **Citizens Advice** offers free help and can advise you on the next steps.

**Shelter** also has free housing advice if you have concerns about your tenancy.
