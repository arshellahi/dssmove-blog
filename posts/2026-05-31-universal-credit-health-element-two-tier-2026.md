If you have a health condition or disability and you are on Universal Credit (UC), an important change took effect on 6 April 2026. The extra payment you can get for your health condition — called the **Limited Capability for Work-Related Activity (LCWRA) element** — now comes in two amounts instead of one.

Which amount you get depends on when you first told DWP about your health condition.

## The two rates — which one are you on?

**Higher rate** — you receive this if, before 6 April 2026, any of the following applied to you:

- You were already getting UC with the LCWRA element included.
- You had already reported your health condition to DWP and were waiting for a Work Capability Assessment (WCA) result.
- You had Employment and Support Allowance (ESA) with the support component and transferred to UC.

**Lower rate** — you receive this if all of these are true:

- You first told DWP about a health condition or disability on or after 6 April 2026.
- DWP assessed you and found you have LCWRA.
- You do not have a severe or permanent condition and you are not terminally ill.

If your condition is very serious, lifelong, or terminal, you should get the higher rate even on a brand-new claim.

## How to check your current amount

Log in to your UC account at GOV.UK. Open your latest statement. The LCWRA element will be listed if you have it, along with the amount for that assessment period.

Not sure what you are looking at? Citizens Advice has a free guide to reading UC statements, and their advisers can walk you through it by phone.

## If you think your amount is wrong

DWP can get decisions wrong. You have the right to challenge one.

The process is called a **mandatory reconsideration**. You have one month from the date of the decision letter to ask DWP to look at it again. Write to them and explain why you think the decision is wrong. You do not need a solicitor.

Citizens Advice can help you write the mandatory reconsideration request at no cost. If the decision is affecting your ability to pay rent, Shelter's free advice line may also be able to help.

## What this change does not affect

Only the LCWRA health element changed. Your standard allowance, housing element, child elements, and childcare costs are not affected.

## Reassessments

DWP may ask you to do a Work Capability Assessment again in future. If you had LCWRA before 6 April 2026 and DWP confirms you still qualify, you should keep the higher rate.
