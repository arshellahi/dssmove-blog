Your tenancy changed on 1 May 2026. If you rent privately in England, your fixed-term contract became a rolling periodic tenancy that day. There is no automatic end date any more. To leave, you need to give proper notice.

## How much notice do you need?

Under the Renters' Rights Act 2025, you must give your landlord at least **2 months' written notice** to end your periodic tenancy. This applies to all private renters in England from 1 May 2026.

Your notice must end on the last day of a rent period. For most people, this means counting back 2 full calendar months from the date your rent is due.

**Example:** your rent is due on the 1st of each month. You want to leave by 1 December. You must serve notice by 1 October — exactly 2 full months before.

## Put it in writing

Give notice in writing — a text, email, or letter all count. Keep a copy with the date on it.

Your notice should include:

- the address of the property
- the date you are giving notice
- the date you plan to leave

A written record protects you if there is ever a dispute about dates.

## Updating your Universal Credit when you move

If you claim Universal Credit, tell your work coach as soon as you serve notice. Then update them again as soon as you have a confirmed new address.

Your housing element covers your rent at your current home until the notice period ends. Keep paying rent in full during those 2 months.

Once you move, update your journal with your new address straight away. A delay can cause a gap in your housing payment.

## Moving to a different area?

Your housing element is worked out using the Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rate for the area you live in. If you move to a different part of England, the rate changes to match your new area.

LHA rates vary a lot between areas. Check the rate for your new area on GOV.UK before you sign a new tenancy agreement. That way, you will know in advance how much of your rent the benefit will cover.

## Can you agree a shorter notice period?

Yes. You and your landlord can agree in writing to end the tenancy in less than 2 months. This must be a genuine agreement — your landlord cannot force you to leave before the notice period ends, and you cannot simply stop paying rent and walk away.

If you need help talking to your landlord, Citizens Advice offers free advice about tenancy rights.

## Keep your rental record clean

If you leave without giving proper notice, you may still owe rent for the remainder of the notice period, even after moving out. Unpaid rent shows as arrears on your rental record and can make it harder to find your next home.

Giving proper written notice is a simple step that protects both your record and your next tenancy.
