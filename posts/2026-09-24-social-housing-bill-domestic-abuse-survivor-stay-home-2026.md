If you are a council or housing association tenant experiencing domestic abuse, Parliament is close to passing a law that could change things for you.

The Social Housing Bill is working its way through Parliament now. Once it becomes law, victims of domestic abuse in social housing will have new rights — including the right to stay in their own home.

## Why this matters

At the moment, getting a perpetrator out of a shared social home is very difficult. If you have a joint tenancy, your landlord can only evict the abuser if the whole tenancy ends. That means you lose the home too.

As a result, many people in England have to give up their council or housing association home each year just to escape domestic abuse.

The Social Housing Bill aims to fix this.

## What will change

**You could stay in your home.** Under the new law, your landlord will be able to apply to court for an order to evict the perpetrator. You would not have to leave first. The court would decide whether the domestic abuse ground for possession has been met.

**The tenancy could move into your name.** The bill creates a way to transfer a joint tenancy into your sole name. Once that happens, the abuser has no legal claim on the home.

**Alternative housing if you need it.** Where it is not safe for you to stay in the property, the court will be able to ask your landlord to offer you suitable social housing nearby. You would not lose your tenancy rights or your place on the housing register.

**Protection against a retaliatory Notice to Quit.** Some perpetrators try to end a joint tenancy by serving a Notice to Quit. This would force both people out of the home. Under the new law, a Notice to Quit served by an abuser will not take effect while domestic abuse possession proceedings are under way. This closes a loophole that has been used as a pressure tactic against victims.

## Getting help now

These changes are not law yet. The bill passed the House of Lords and had its second reading in the House of Commons in September 2026. It is expected to become law in the coming months.

If you are experiencing domestic abuse in your home right now, do not wait. Contact Shelter or Citizens Advice — both offer free, confidential advice. Your local council housing team must also help you if you are at risk of becoming homeless because of domestic abuse.
