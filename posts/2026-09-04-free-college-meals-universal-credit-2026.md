## What changed in September 2026

Until this term, your teenager at college could only get free meals if your household earned £7,400 or less a year — even if you claimed Universal Credit (UC).

That earnings limit has now been removed.

From September 2026, any family claiming UC qualifies for free meals at a state-funded further education college or sixth form — no matter how much you earn.

This is part of the same government expansion that extended free school meals to older school children from September 2026. It now covers 16 to 19 year olds in further education too.

## Who qualifies

Your teenager can get free college meals from September 2026 if:

- They are aged 16 to 19
- They attend a college or sixth form funded by the Department for Education
- Your household claims Universal Credit

Your household income does not matter. UC is the qualifying benefit.

The rule does not apply to private providers or independent colleges. If you are unsure whether your teenager's college is state-funded, ask their student services office.

## How much you could save

A college lunch typically costs £3 to £5 a day. Over a full academic year that adds up to around £500 or more per student.

If you have 2 teenagers at college, you could save over £1,000 a year. That is money that can go towards your rent, bills, and food at home.

## How to apply

Free meals do not start automatically. Your teenager needs to apply at their college.

Here is what to do:

1. **Contact the college's student services office.** Ask them how to apply for free meals under Universal Credit.
2. **Show proof of your UC award.** This is usually a recent award notice, a letter from DWP, or a screenshot of your online UC account.
3. **Apply this week.** The new term has already started. The sooner you apply, the sooner your teenager starts receiving free meals.

The college checks eligibility and registers your teenager. Most colleges can process this quickly.

## If your teenager was already getting free meals

Some students received free meals under older transitional rules that were in place since 2018. Those protections ended at the end of the 2025 to 2026 academic year.

If your teenager was getting free meals last year, check with their college now. They may need to reapply to confirm they still qualify under the new rules. If your household claims UC, they should still be eligible — but only if they reapply.

Do not assume last year's arrangement carries over. A quick message to the college avoids your teenager missing out on meals they are entitled to.
