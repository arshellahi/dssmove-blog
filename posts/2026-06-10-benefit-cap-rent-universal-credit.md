If you claim Universal Credit, there is a limit on how much you can receive in total each month. It is called the benefit cap. When your benefits hit the cap, DWP cuts your housing element first — so your rent shortfall can grow quickly.

## What the cap limits are

Outside Greater London, the cap is £1,835 a month for couples and lone parents, and £1,229 a month for single adults.

Inside Greater London, the cap is higher at £2,110 a month for couples and lone parents, and £1,413 a month for single adults.

These limits cover your whole UC award — standard allowance, housing element, child elements, and most other additions added together.

## Who is automatically exempt

The cap does not apply to you if any of the following are true:

- You or your partner get the Limited Capability for Work and Work-Related Activity (LCWRA) element of Universal Credit.
- You or your partner get the carer element of Universal Credit, Carer's Allowance, or Carer Support Payment.
- Your combined earnings are at least £881 a month after tax. This threshold rose from £846 on 1 April 2026.
- You are of pension age.

The LCWRA element is the extra UC payment for people with a health condition that limits their ability to work. If you think you qualify but have not yet claimed it, write in your UC journal to start the process.

## What to do if you are capped

When you are capped, you will see a "benefit cap" deduction line in your UC payment statement. Here is what to do:

**Check if you qualify for an exemption.** If your health, caring responsibilities, or earnings have changed, you may now be exempt. Contact Citizens Advice at citizensadvice.org.uk for help checking.

**Apply for a Crisis and Resilience Fund Housing Payment.** Your council can top up the gap between your reduced UC and your rent. Search for "Crisis and Resilience Fund Housing Payment" plus your council name to find their form.

**Ask about the nine-month grace period.** If you have recently lost work and your earnings drop below £881 a month, you can ask for a nine-month grace period before the cap kicks in. Tell your work coach straight away.

## How to check if you are affected

Sign in to your Universal Credit account and look at your most recent payment breakdown. If you see a deduction labelled "benefit cap," that is the amount being removed. You can also use the free calculator at turn2us.org.uk to see whether the cap is likely to affect your claim.

If you are capped and think the deduction is wrong, Citizens Advice can help you challenge it.
