If you get Personal Independence Payment (PIP) or the health element of Universal Credit (UC), you may have worried that trying work would put your payments at risk. New rules in force since 30 April 2026 give you legal protection when you give work a try.

## What the Right to Try is

The new rules say that starting paid work — or taking on voluntary work — will not, on its own, cause DWP to review your PIP award or carry out a new Work Capability Assessment (WCA). This is called the **Right to Try**.

Before this change, many disabled people and those with health conditions stayed away from work altogether. They were afraid that trying even a few hours would trigger a full reassessment. That fear is now removed by law.

## Who is covered

You can use the Right to Try if you:

- get PIP at any rate
- have been assessed as having Limited Capability for Work (LCW) or Limited Capability for Work-Related Activity (LCWRA) on your UC claim
- receive New Style Employment and Support Allowance (ESA) and are in the Work Related Activity Group or the Support Group

The rules do not apply if you are still waiting for your first Work Capability Assessment.

## What you can do

You can start a paid job — full or part time — or try volunteering. You choose the pace. If the work does not suit you or your health, you can stop. Your benefit carries on at the same rate and no reassessment is automatically triggered.

This gives you real freedom to explore work without gambling your income.

## What the rules do not change

DWP can still review your benefit if:

- your condition has improved significantly
- your circumstances have changed in another way
- a planned regular review is already due
- fraud is suspected

If you are unsure how starting work could affect you, speak to Citizens Advice before you begin.

## Report your earnings

The Right to Try does not remove your duty to report earnings. Tell DWP about any wages through your UC journal or by calling the Universal Credit helpline. Your housing element will adjust based on your income — but you will not lose your housing support simply for trying work.

## Why this matters for renters

Many people who rent on Universal Credit have a health condition. Being able to try a few hours of work — without immediately risking your housing element — gives you more ways to top up your income. This matters especially if your Local Housing Allowance rate does not cover your full rent.
