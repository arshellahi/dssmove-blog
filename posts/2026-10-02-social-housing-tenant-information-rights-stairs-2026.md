From 1 October 2026, if you rent from a housing association in England, you have important new rights.

Your landlord must now publish key information about how they manage your home. You do not have to ask for it — they must make it available.

This is called a **publication scheme**, and it is part of new rules set by the Regulator of Social Housing (RSH).

## What must they publish?

Housing associations must now publish information covering areas such as:

- how they handle repairs
- how they manage complaints
- their approach to antisocial behaviour
- their policies on how staff should behave

If your housing association is not doing these things well, the published information helps you spot the gap and challenge them.

## What changes in April 2027?

From **April 2027**, you can go further. You will be able to formally ask your housing association for specific information about how your home is managed. They must respond within a set time.

This brings housing association tenants closer to the rights that council tenants have already had for some time.

## New professional standards for housing association staff

The same rules also introduce a new Competence and Conduct Standard. From 1 October 2026, housing associations must make sure their staff have the right skills, knowledge, and behaviours to treat you with respect.

Senior managers and executives must have — or be working towards — a relevant professional qualification. Large housing associations (those with 1,000 or more homes) have 3 years to meet this. Smaller ones have 4 years.

You can ask your housing association what they are doing to meet these standards.

## What if things go wrong?

If your housing association is not following the new rules, or is treating you badly, you have options:

- **Housing Ombudsman** — investigates complaints about social landlords for free. You can contact them directly or through their website.
- **Citizens Advice** — can help you put your complaint in writing and explain what you are entitled to.
- **Regulator of Social Housing** — you can report serious failings to them if your landlord is not meeting the standards.

Use the information your housing association publishes as evidence if things go wrong. Knowing your rights makes you a stronger tenant.
