Many people on Universal Credit (UC), Personal Independence Payment (PIP) or Employment and Support Allowance (ESA) would like to try paid work. But fear of losing their benefits has held them back.

A DWP survey found that 37% of disabled people and those with long-term health conditions who want to work said fear of losing benefits was stopping them. A new legal protection — called the Right to Try — changes that.

## What is the Right to Try?

From 30 April 2026, trying a paid job or doing voluntary work will not, on its own, trigger a reassessment of your benefit award.

That matters because a reassessment can sometimes reduce or end your payments. The Right to Try means working or volunteering alone is no longer enough reason for DWP to review your UC, PIP, or new-style ESA.

## Who does it cover?

The Right to Try applies to 3 benefits:

- **Universal Credit** — specifically if you receive the health element (also called LCWRA)
- **Personal Independence Payment** (PIP)
- **New-style Employment and Support Allowance** (ESA)

If you receive any of these, you can try a job or start volunteering without automatically putting your award at risk.

## What counts as trying work?

The protection covers both starting a paid job and taking on unpaid voluntary work.

If you try work and it does not suit you — for example because your health gets worse or the role is too demanding — your benefits are not automatically taken away. The Right to Try is meant to give you a safe way to test what you can manage.

## What you should still do

The Right to Try does not mean you can ignore your claim. Keep these steps in mind:

- **Tell your work coach** at the Jobcentre before you start. They can record it on your claim.
- **Update your UC journal** if you claim UC. Add your work details as prompted.
- **Get advice first** from Citizens Advice if you are unsure how any extra income from work might affect your housing costs, council tax reduction, or other benefits.

## Why this matters for your rent

Even a small amount of extra income from a trial job can help cover rent shortfalls. And with the Right to Try in place, you have more security if things do not go to plan.

If you want to understand how trying work could affect your full benefit package, get free advice at citizensadvice.org.uk.
