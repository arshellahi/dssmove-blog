As autumn approaches, it is worth knowing about a cash payment you could receive automatically when the weather turns cold. If you get Universal Credit (UC), you may be entitled to a **Cold Weather Payment** every time there is a freezing cold spell — with no application needed.

The scheme runs from **1 November 2026** to **31 March 2027**.

## How much is a Cold Weather Payment?

You get **£25** for every 7-day period when the average temperature at your local weather station is recorded as, or forecast to be, 0°C or below.

The money goes straight into the same bank account as your other benefit payments. You do not have to do anything — it is paid automatically, usually within 14 working days of each cold period.

## Who qualifies if they get Universal Credit?

You need to be getting UC and at least one of the following must apply:

- You and your partner are not employed or gainfully self-employed.
- You or your partner have a health condition or disability and have been found to have **limited capability for work** — this covers both the LCW and LCWRA groups.
- Your UC claim includes a **disabled child amount**.

If you are in paid work but do not have a health condition or a disabled child in your claim, you usually will not qualify.

Cold Weather Payments are also available to people receiving Pension Credit, Income Support, income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA), or income-based Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA).

## How will I know if I have been paid?

You do not need to log in or make a claim. DWP checks your postcode against a network of local weather stations and triggers the payment automatically. Check your bank statement a couple of weeks after any cold period and look for a payment from DWP.

## What if I should have been paid but have not?

First, check your bank account — the payment can take up to 14 working days to arrive.

If it still has not arrived after that time, log in to your Universal Credit account and add a note to your journal. Tell DWP the dates of the cold period you believe triggered the payment.

If you are not sure whether you qualify, Citizens Advice can check your entitlement for free. They also offer a full benefits check in case you are missing anything else you are entitled to.

## How many payments could I get this winter?

That depends on how many cold spells happen in your local area. In a mild winter you might get nothing. In a harsher winter with several cold snaps, you could receive two, three, or more payments — each one worth £25.

Not every area triggers at the same time. DWP uses the weather station closest to your postcode, so your area's payments may differ from someone living nearby in a different station zone.

## What can you do now?

You do not need to do anything in advance of the scheme opening. It is worth checking that your Universal Credit account shows your correct address and bank details so that any payment goes to the right place.

If you are unsure which UC group you are in, contact Citizens Advice or check your UC to-do list before winter arrives.
