## What is changing?

If you live in supported housing or temporary accommodation, there is good news. New rules from 5 October 2026 mean you can earn more from work without your Housing Benefit being cut.

The rules are called the Housing Benefit (Earned Income Disregards) Regulations 2026. Parliament approved them on 6 July 2026.

At the moment, Housing Benefit and Universal Credit (UC) treat earnings differently. UC has more generous work allowances — the amount you can earn before your benefit drops. Housing Benefit for people in supported housing has lower allowances. This creates a cliff edge. If you start a job or increase your hours, your Housing Benefit can fall sharply, leaving you no better off.

From October 2026, five new earned income disregards will be added to Housing Benefit for people in supported housing and temporary accommodation. An earned income disregard is the amount of your wages that does not count when your benefit is worked out. The more that is disregarded, the more of your pay you keep.

## Who does this apply to?

This applies to you if you live in:

- **supported housing** — including hostels, refuges, and housing with care or supervision
- **temporary accommodation** placed by your council — such as a bed and breakfast or short-term flat while you wait to be rehoused

Around 315,000 people in Great Britain are expected to benefit.

These rules do **not** apply to standard private rented homes. If you rent privately and get UC, your housing costs already follow UC's own work allowance rules.

## Why this matters to you

If you are in temporary accommodation and want to move into private rental, starting work is a key step. These rules mean you can do that without putting your housing support at risk.

Before, some people in supported housing felt they had to choose between staying out of work or risking their Housing Benefit. The old rules could leave you no better off if you took on paid work. These new rules remove that problem.

No one is made worse off by this change. If you are already working in supported housing, your situation can only stay the same or get better.

## What to do next

Talk to your keyworker or housing support team. Ask them how the October 2026 changes will affect your benefit.

If you are in temporary accommodation, contact your council's housing team. They can confirm whether these rules apply to your situation.

You can also get free advice from [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk). They can help you understand what to expect and plan your next steps.
