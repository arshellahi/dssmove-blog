If you pay your own water bill and claim Universal Credit, you may be able to cap what you pay each month. A scheme called WaterSure caps your bill at the average metered rate in your water company's area. Rules changed in 2026 — the biggest update in nearly 30 years — and more people now qualify.

## What WaterSure does

WaterSure caps your water bill at the average amount paid by metered households in your water company's area. If your household uses more than that — because you have several children or a health condition — you still pay only up to that average. You will not be charged extra for high usage when your needs are genuinely high.

## Who can apply

You need to meet 3 conditions.

**First**, you must have a water meter fitted at your home, or you must be on the waiting list for one to be installed.

**Second**, you or your partner must receive a qualifying benefit. Universal Credit (UC) counts. Housing Benefit, Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA), Employment and Support Allowance (ESA), Income Support, and Pension Credit also qualify. From 2026, some disability benefits such as Personal Independence Payment (PIP) may also count — contact your water company to check your specific situation.

**Third**, your household must use a large amount of water. You qualify on this condition if:

- you have 3 or more children under 19 in full-time education living at home, or
- someone in your home has a medical condition that causes high water use — for example, kidney disease, incontinence, or a skin condition requiring frequent washing. From 2026, you no longer need to provide a medical note to prove this — your water company will take a self-declaration instead.

## How to apply

Call your water company directly and ask about WaterSure. Have a recent Universal Credit statement or benefit letter ready as evidence. If you qualify, the cap applies from the beginning of your current billing period.

Many water companies also run their own extra help schemes alongside WaterSure. It is always worth asking about these too when you call — you may be able to get additional support.

## What if you do not have a meter?

Ask your water company to fit a meter free of charge. You can apply for WaterSure while you are waiting for the meter to be installed, so do not wait.

## Scotland and Northern Ireland

WaterSure covers metered homes in England and Wales. If you live in Scotland or Northern Ireland, contact your local council or housing service to find out what low-income water support is available where you are.

## Get help

If you are not sure whether you qualify, Citizens Advice can help you check your eligibility and what evidence to gather. Find your nearest service at the Citizens Advice website or use their online chat.
