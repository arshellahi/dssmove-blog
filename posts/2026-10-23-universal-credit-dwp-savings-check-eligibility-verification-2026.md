## DWP can now ask banks to check your savings

DWP has a new way to check that benefit payments are going to the right people. It is called the Eligibility Verification Measure (EVM).

Under the EVM, DWP can send a request to your bank. The bank checks whether your account holds more than the Universal Credit (UC) savings limit. That limit is £16,000.

This is not the same as DWP reading your bank statements. Here is what it actually means for you.

## The savings rules for Universal Credit

UC has two key savings thresholds:

- **Under £6,000** — savings below this amount are ignored. They do not change how much UC you get.
- **£6,000 to £16,000** — savings in this range reduce your UC by a small amount each month. The more you save in this band, the less UC you receive.
- **Over £16,000** — you usually cannot claim UC at all.

Your home, and money saved in a pension, does not count as savings under these rules.

You must tell DWP if your savings go up. Do this through your UC journal or by speaking to your work coach.

## What DWP can and cannot see

When a bank responds to a DWP request, it shares only basic account information. For example, it may confirm whether an account holds more than £16,000.

DWP cannot see:

- your bank statements
- what you spend your money on
- your transactions, direct debits, or standing orders

The bank does not tell DWP that you are the claimant. It simply checks whether the account meets a set condition.

## Your rights if your account is flagged

If a bank flags your account, DWP will look into it. No automatic action will be taken against you.

A DWP caseworker — not a computer — will always review any case before a decision is made. You will get a chance to share information and explain your situation.

The EVM currently applies to Universal Credit, Pension Credit, and New Style Employment and Support Allowance (ESA).

## What to do now

If your savings are under £6,000 and you have already told DWP about them, you do not need to do anything.

If your savings have gone up recently, update DWP as soon as possible. Use your UC journal, call the UC helpline, or tell your work coach.

If DWP contacts you about your claim, or you are not sure how your savings affect your benefit, get free advice from Citizens Advice. Visit [citizensadvice.org.uk](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/) or find your nearest office at the same address.
