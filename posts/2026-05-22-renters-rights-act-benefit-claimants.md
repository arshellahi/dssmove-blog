From 1 May 2026, a new law called the Renters' Rights Act gives private tenants in England stronger protections. If you claim Universal Credit (UC) or Housing Benefit, 2 of the biggest changes affect you directly.

## Landlords cannot turn you away for being on benefits

For years, many tenants on benefits faced "No DSS" policies. These were blanket refusals by landlords who would not rent to anyone claiming benefits. From 1 May 2026, this practice is banned.

A landlord cannot refuse to rent to you just because you claim UC or Housing Benefit. They can still check whether you can afford the rent. But your source of income cannot be the reason they say no.

The same rule covers parents. A landlord cannot refuse you just because you have children.

If a landlord or letting agent turns you away because of your benefits, you now have grounds to challenge that. Citizens Advice can help you understand your options at no cost.

## No more no-fault evictions

Before May 2026, a landlord could hand you a section 21 notice and ask you to leave without giving a reason. Section 21 no longer exists.

If a landlord wants you to leave, they must now have a specific legal reason. Common grounds include serious rent arrears or the landlord needing the property back to live in. They must serve a section 8 notice instead.

If you receive any eviction notice and are unsure what it means, contact Citizens Advice or Shelter straight away. Both offer free advice.

## Your tenancy is now rolling

If you had an assured shorthold tenancy before 1 May 2026, it automatically became an assured periodic tenancy. That means it now runs month to month with no fixed end date. Your landlord cannot end your tenancy simply because a short fixed term has run out.

## Rent in advance

When you sign a new tenancy agreement, your landlord can ask for up to 1 month's rent in advance. They cannot ask you for money before you have signed.

## The information sheet

Your landlord must give you a copy of the official Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet by 31 May 2026. It explains your new rights in plain language. You can also download it from GOV.UK yourself if you want to read it now.

## Where to get help

- **Citizens Advice** — free advice on your rights and how to challenge discrimination.
- **Shelter** — free helpline and online guides, including specialist help if you face eviction.
